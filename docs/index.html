Two reviews

No skillplain prompt

It went politely, but the call ended without a real next step. Alex and Nina exchanged pleasantries, and nothing was locked in.

What went wrong:
- No date or time. "Soon" and "talk soon" mean nothing. Neither side has a reason to act by a specific day.
- No agreed next meeting. Alex is sending a proposal, and Nina will "take a look." Nobody has booked a review call to walk through it.
- Nina's commitment is vague. "This all makes sense to me" is agreement with the conversation, not with a decision or a purchase. She didn't name who else needs to sign off, what would make the proposal acceptable, or when she plans to decide.
- Alex didn't confirm the basics. There's no check on budget, timeline, or who else is involved, so the proposal may miss the mark and stall.
- Alex made all the commitments. The only firm action on the call is Alex's "I'll send it." If it's late or generic, the deal goes quiet.

Stronger close (example):
> "Before I send the proposal, can we agree on what would make it a good fit for you? [Confirm the two or three things she cares most about.] Then I'd like to book 30 minutes on Thursday the 22nd to walk through it together. Who else needs to be in that conversation? And what does your decision process look like after that?"

Then get a calendar invite sent before hanging up, and send the proposal within 24 hours of the call so the review meeting has a clear purpose.

The transcript is short and labeled synthetic, so I can only judge the ending. If the earlier part of the call covered budget, timeline, and decision-makers, the issue is narrower: Alex just didn't turn that into a dated next step.

What differs

The plain run finds the same gaps on this call. That is the honest result. What the skill adds is a fixed shape: one verdict from three named elements, the exact words that were said, what is missing, and one rewrite. The plain run wanders into budget, timeline and who else is involved, which this question does not ask. A fixed shape is what lets you compare a hundred calls and trust the comparison.

The skills also follow three evidence rules in every audit: the buyer must say it, a topic the call never touched scores Missed, and each dimension lists what does not count.
