# Partner-Competition-Landscape rubric

This rubric has 6 dimensions. It audits a call with a channel or reseller partner. The partner is the other side of the call, so partner words count the way buyer words count in a sales call.

Three rules apply to every dimension.

- **The partner must say it.** Evidence is something the partner said. A claim that only the vendor's rep makes, such as "they love us", does not count. Partner size and reputation are not evidence of fit. A claim that only the rep makes does not count.
- **Absence is a result.** A dimension that the call never touched is Missed. Do not credit it because the call felt friendly.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each dimension lists the usual false positives.


## 1. Rivals the partner carries

**What it is.** The partner names the other vendors they sell.

**Covered:** the partner names the rivals. **Partial:** the partner says "a few others". **Missed:** no rival comes up.

**Does not count:** a rival that only the rep names. A vendor the partner dropped long ago, unless the partner says why.

**A strong call** asks which vendors they sell for the same use case.

## 2. Share of their business

**What it is.** The partner states how much each vendor is.

**Covered:** the partner gives a share or a count for each rival. **Partial:** the partner says one is bigger. **Missed:** no share comes up.

**Does not count:** "mostly" with nothing else. A share that only the rep guesses.

**A strong call** asks for the share of revenue or deals for each vendor.

## 3. What the rivals offer that the vendor does not

**What it is.** The partner names specific differences.

**Covered:** the partner names a specific capability and says customers ask for it. **Partial:** the partner says the rival is "better". **Missed:** no difference comes up.

**Does not count:** praise for a rival. A difference that only the rep names.

**A strong call** asks what customers ask for that the vendor lacks.

## 4. How terms compare

**What it is.** The partner states how margin and terms compare.

**Covered:** the partner states the margin or term difference with a figure. **Partial:** the partner says a rival is "a bit higher" with no figure. **Missed:** terms do not come up.

**Does not count:** "I do not remember" with no follow-up. Terms that only the rep states.

**A strong call** asks the exact margin and where the partner can find the number.

## 5. What would make the partner switch a customer

**What it is.** The partner states a condition for moving a customer.

**Covered:** the partner names a condition and a case where it happened. **Partial:** the partner says they might. **Missed:** no switch condition comes up.

**Does not count:** "if a customer asked" with no case. A hypothetical that only the rep raises.

**A strong call** asks for the last time they moved a customer between vendors, and why.

## 6. Conflict or exclusivity rules

**What it is.** The partner says whether any rules limit carrying rivals.

**Covered:** the partner states the rules, or states that none exist and who confirmed it. **Partial:** the partner says "not that I know of". **Missed:** rules do not come up.

**Does not count:** "not that I know of" with no check. A rule that only the rep assumes.

**A strong call** asks who confirms the rules and gets the answer in writing.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize an audit.

- A call that covered every dimension with the partner's own words must not come back mostly Missed. When it does, widen what counts as evidence.
- A call that never raised a dimension must not get credit for it. When a status rests on a line from a "does not count" list, remove that status.

## Sources

The dimensions follow common channel-management practice. The evidence rules and the strong-call lines come from reviews of recorded partner and sales calls.
