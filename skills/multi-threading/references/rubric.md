# Multi-Threading rubric

This rubric has six dimensions. They show whether a deal rests on one contact or on a buying group that the rep mapped and reached.

Three rules apply to every dimension.

- **The buyer must say it.** Evidence is something the buyer said about the people involved. A name that only the CRM holds, or a role that only the rep assumes, does not count. A claim that only the rep makes does not count.
- **Absence is a result.** A dimension that the call never touched is Missed. Do not credit it because the call felt friendly.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each dimension lists the usual false positives.


## 1. Buying group mapped

**What it is.** The buyer names the people involved and their roles in the decision.

**Covered:** the buyer names the people who recommend, approve, and are consulted. **Partial:** the buyer refers to "my team" or "leadership" with no names. **Missed:** the call never learns who else is involved.

**Does not count:** titles from the CRM. People that the rep guesses are involved.

**A strong call** asks who recommends, who approves, and who must be consulted.

## 2. Approver reached

**What it is.** The person who approves the spend is named and joined a call or has a dated step.

**Covered:** the approver is named and joined a call or has a dated step in the plan. **Partial:** the approver is named and has not been reached. **Missed:** nobody learns who approves.

**Does not count:** seniority. The contact saying they will "update their boss". A weekly update that the contact gives.

**A strong call** asks the contact to bring the approver to the next call.

## 3. Technical evaluator reached

**What it is.** The person who judges the technical fit is named and engaged.

**Covered:** a named technical owner has spoken on a call or has a dated step. **Partial:** IT or a technical team is mentioned and no person is named. **Missed:** no technical person appears on a deal where one matters.

**Does not count:** "IT will look at it at some point". A technical person who is on the call and whom the buyer does not name as an evaluator.

**A strong call** asks who judges the integration and when they can join.

## 4. Daily users heard

**What it is.** The people who use the product every day are heard.

**Covered:** a named daily user spoke on a call or gave feedback that the buyer reports. **Partial:** the buyer describes the users with no name and no feedback. **Missed:** the users never come up.

**Does not count:** the contact speaking for the whole team. "My team likes it" with no name.

**A strong call** asks to hear from the day-to-day lead for ten minutes.

## 5. Second internal advocate

**What it is.** A second person, besides the first contact, acts for the deal.

**Covered:** a second named person has taken an action for the deal, such as raising it in a meeting. **Partial:** a second person is positive and has done nothing. **Missed:** only one person pushes the deal.

**Does not count:** friendliness or seniority. A person whom others wait on for sign-off, who is the approver and not an advocate.

**A strong call** asks who else benefits and what they have already done.

## 6. A dated plan to broaden

**What it is.** A dated step exists to bring a named person into the deal.

**Covered:** the buyer accepts a dated step to bring a named person in. **Partial:** the buyer says they will think about it. **Missed:** no step is discussed.

**Does not count:** "we will loop them in sometime". A date that the rep proposes and the buyer never accepts.

**A strong call** books the introduction with a date and an agenda.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize an audit.

- A deal with the approver, a technical owner, and a user all reached, and a dated step for the rest, must not come back mostly Missed. When it does, widen what counts as evidence.
- A call that never raised a dimension must not get credit for it. When a status rests on a line from a "does not count" list, remove that status.

## Sources

The dimensions follow common multi-threading and buying-group practice. The evidence rules and the strong-call lines come from reviews of recorded sales calls.
