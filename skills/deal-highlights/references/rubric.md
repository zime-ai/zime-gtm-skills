# What makes a highlight

Six types count. Everything else is noise, no matter how long it runs.

1. **A real commitment** — either side names a concrete action with a date
   or trigger attached ("I'll get budget approval by Friday", "we'll send
   the security questionnaire this week"). A vague intent ("we should
   circle back at some point") is not a commitment — skip it, or if it's
   the closest thing said and nothing better exists, don't upgrade it into
   a commitment line just to hit a count.
2. **An objection** — the prospect pushes back on price, timeline, fit,
   internal buy-in, or anything else that could stall or kill the deal.
3. **A buying signal** — the prospect asks about implementation,
   onboarding, contract terms, or otherwise acts like they're already
   picturing using the product.
4. **A competitive mention** — a named competitor, or an unnamed "we're
   also looking at other vendors" that changes how the deal should be
   read.
5. **A risk or blocker** — a stakeholder who isn't bought in, a budget
   freeze, a procurement hurdle, a technical dealbreaker.
6. **A number that matters** — budget, timeline, headcount, seat count,
   contract value — anything with a figure attached that changes the
   deal's shape.

## What disqualifies a candidate line

- **No quote or timestamp behind it.** The evidence rule is absolute: if
  you can't point to where it was said, it doesn't go in the list. This
  applies equally to all six types above — a commitment without a quote is
  worth exactly as little as an objection without one.
- **Generic rapport-building.** Small talk, scheduling logistics, "nice to
  meet you" — even a long stretch of it — is not a highlight just because
  it took up call time.
- **Restating the same highlight twice.** If the same commitment or
  objection comes up more than once in the call, cite the clearest
  instance once, not every mention.

## Output discipline

Don't pad to a target count. A short, thin call earns a short list — 2-3
items is a legitimate output, not a failure to find more. A rich call can
run past 10 if every extra line is a genuine type-1-through-6 moment with
its own quote; the 5-10 range in `SKILL.md` is typical, not a hard cap.
