# Upsell & expansion rubric

Eight dimensions for an expansion or upsell conversation. The account's
underlying health comes first — an expansion pushed onto a wobbling account
is a bigger risk than any gap in the pitch itself.

Three rules apply to every dimension.

- **The customer must say it.** Evidence is something the customer said, or a figure from their own data that they accept on the call. A claim that only the rep makes, such as "they love it", does not count.
- **Absence is a result.** A dimension that the call never touched is Missed. Do not credit it because the tone was warm.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each dimension lists the usual false positives.

Expansion means new scope that the customer does not have today, such as new users, teams, or products, and the customer talks about money. More use inside what the customer already pays for is not expansion.

## 1. Account health check-in first

**What it is.** Whether the call surfaces any renewal risk, unresolved
product issue, or fading champion before anything else gets scored.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like an explicit health statement,
positive or negative, made before the expansion pitch is scored. A miss
looks like health going unaddressed while the conversation jumps straight
to the upsell.

**Weak:** "Things seem fine, moving on to the expansion." **Strong:** "No
renewal risk surfaced — usage is steady across the team and the champion
is engaged — so it's reasonable to proceed with the expansion read."

**Does not count:** "things seem fine". Praise from a general user. Early pilot feedback, which is not adoption.

**A strong call** states the health of the account with a usage figure, labeled healthy, under-used, or over-used.

## 2. The expansion ask is concrete

**What it is.** A specific use case, team, or product line driving the
expansion ask, not a generic "you should buy more."

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a named use case or team tied to
the ask. A miss looks like the ask staying generic with no specific driver.

**Weak:** "They might want more seats eventually." **Strong:** "Their
support team of six wants access after seeing how the sales team uses call
coaching."

**Does not count:** "they might want more eventually". More use inside the current plan. A feature request with no link to new scope.

**A strong call** names the team or product that wants the new scope, and what they will use it for.

## 3. Champion strength for this ask

**What it is.** Whether the internal champion has the standing and
motivation to actually advocate for this expansion internally, not just
personal interest.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like evidence of the champion's
internal standing or a stated intent to advocate. A miss looks like
champion enthusiasm noted with nothing about their ability to move it
internally.

**Weak:** "Our contact likes the idea." **Strong:** "Our champion said
she'll bring this to her VP's budget review next week — she's done that
successfully for two other tools."

**Does not count:** friendliness or enthusiasm. Seniority. Action defines a champion, such as taking the ask to a budget review.

**A strong call** asks what the champion has already done for this ask and what they need next.

## 4. Confidence read

**What it is.** How likely the expansion feels to actually close, grounded
in something specific from the call rather than general optimism.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a confidence read tied to a
specific signal (budget confirmed, timeline named). A miss looks like
optimism asserted with nothing behind it.

**Weak:** "Feels promising." **Strong:** "They confirmed budget is already
allocated for Q2 headcount tools, which is a stronger signal than general
interest."

**Does not count:** "feels promising". A signal that only the rep reads into the tone.

**A strong call** ties the confidence to one stated signal, such as allocated budget or a named date.

## 5. Strategic alignment

**What it is.** Whether the expansion ties into something the customer's
leadership is actively prioritizing this year, versus being opportunistic
with no larger story.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a named leadership priority tied
to the expansion. A miss looks like no connection to a larger initiative
mentioned.

**Weak:** "It would help the team." **Strong:** "This directly supports the
efficiency initiative their COO announced last quarter."

**Does not count:** "it would help the team". A priority that the rep names for the customer's leadership.

**A strong call** asks which leadership goal the expansion supports this year.

## 6. Open risks or product issues named

**What it is.** Any unresolved product issue or open complaint that should
be addressed before or alongside the expansion ask.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like an open issue named explicitly,
even if minor. A miss looks like open issues going unmentioned while the
pitch proceeds.

**Weak:** "No issues to report." **Strong:** "There's still an open ticket
on the export bug — worth resolving before asking them to expand usage."

**Does not count:** a routine feature request. A specialist tool the customer tried for a narrow job. That is normal and is a risk only when the customer ties the job to the reason they bought.

**A strong call** names each open issue with an owner and a date.

## 7. Enablement gap check

**What it is.** Whether uneven usage of the existing product is explained
by a training gap (fixable, possibly part of the expansion pitch) or a fit
problem (a reason to pause, not expand).

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like an explicit read on which of the
two it is. A miss looks like uneven usage noted with no explanation.

**Weak:** "Usage could be higher." **Strong:** "The gap is a training issue
— their new hires never got onboarded to the core workflow, not a sign the
product doesn't fit."

**Does not count:** "they could use more training maybe". A gap that the rep guesses with no sign from the customer.

**A strong call** names who missed onboarding and what they need.

## 8. Clear next step

**What it is.** A specific next step — a proposal date, a stakeholder to
loop in — produced by the call.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a named date or owner for the
next step. A miss looks like general interest with nothing scheduled.

**Weak:** "We'll follow up." **Strong:** "Proposal goes to their VP by
Friday, with their ops lead looped in for technical sign-off."

**Does not count:** "let us keep talking". A date that the rep proposes and the customer never accepts.

**A strong call** ends with a dated next step, its owner, and the people who must attend.

## Reads-well-too test

Sanity-check against an expansion call you know led to a healthy close. If
it still comes back mostly Missed, loosen the read before reporting — but
keep dimension 1 strict either way: when genuinely unsure whether an
account is healthy enough to expand, say so rather than defaulting to
"probably fine."
