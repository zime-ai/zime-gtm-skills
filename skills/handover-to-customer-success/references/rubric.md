# Handover-to-Customer-Success rules

Writes the handover from the sales calls. Its main job is to catch what was promised on the calls, because a promise that CS never hears about becomes the first complaint. It uses nothing from outside the transcripts.

Three rules apply to every section.

- **Cite or leave it out.** Every claim traces to a quote, a timestamp, or a column and cell. A claim with no cited source does not ship.
- **The buyer or customer must say it.** A claim that only the rep makes does not count unless the other side accepted it. Silence is not a yes.
- **Unknown stays unknown.** When the input does not say it, write "not stated". Do not fill the gap with a guess or with outside knowledge.

## Why they bought

State the problem and the reason to act now in the customer's words. Quote it. Do not restate the pitch.

## Success measure

State the measure that the customer named, with the target and the date. When none was named, write "not stated" and say who should ask.

## People and roles

List each person who spoke or was named, with the role that the customer gave. Mark the approver and the day-to-day owner. Note stated limits, such as available hours.

## What was promised

List every promise made on the calls, by either side, with the speaker and a quote. Mark each as in scope now, planned later, or unclear. A feature that is planned is not delivered.

## Gaps and open items

List what is not ready, not confirmed, or not answered. Give each an owner and a date when the calls name them. Otherwise write "no owner" or "no date". Include any gap between what the customer understood and what was true.

## Key dates

List dates that the customer or the rep stated, such as go-live, training, and review dates. Mark each as confirmed by both sides, or as the rep's plan only.

## What does not count

- A promise that the rep makes and the customer never heard.
- A go-live date that nobody on the delivery side confirmed, written as fixed.
- A planned feature written as included now.
- A success measure that the note's writer invents.
- Anything about the customer from outside the calls.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize.

- A deal with a clear problem, a named measure, named people, and clear promises must produce a clean note. Do not add risks that the calls do not support.
- A thin input must not get a padded output. When the input supports two items, write two. Do not invent a third to reach a count.

## Sources

The structure follows common sales-to-CS handoff practice. The rules come from reviews of recorded sales calls.
