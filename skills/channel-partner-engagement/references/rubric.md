# Channel-Partner-Engagement rubric

This rubric has 6 dimensions. It audits a call with a channel or reseller partner. The partner is the other side of the call, so partner words count the way buyer words count in a sales call.

Three rules apply to every dimension.

- **The partner must say it.** Evidence is something the partner said. A claim that only the vendor's rep makes, such as "they love us", does not count. Partner size and reputation are not evidence of fit. A claim that only the rep makes does not count.
- **Absence is a result.** A dimension that the call never touched is Missed. Do not credit it because the call felt friendly.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each dimension lists the usual false positives.


## 1. Current engagement in the partner's figures

**What it is.** The partner states how active they are, with counts.

**Covered:** the partner gives counts, such as deals worked or closed, for a stated period. **Partial:** the partner says "active" or "quiet" with no count. **Missed:** no engagement level comes up.

**Does not count:** "going well" with no count. The rep's own view of activity.

**A strong call** asks how many deals they worked and closed in the last two quarters.

## 2. Capacity to sell

**What it is.** The partner says who sells the product and how much time they have.

**Covered:** the partner names people and the share of their time. **Partial:** the partner says "my team". **Missed:** no capacity comes up.

**Does not count:** headcount of the whole company. A person named with no time stated.

**A strong call** asks who sells it and how many hours a week each person has.

## 3. Customers they could reach

**What it is.** The partner names the customers or segment they could take the product to.

**Covered:** the partner names a segment and a count of customers. **Partial:** the partner says "our clients". **Missed:** no reach comes up.

**Does not count:** partner size or fame. A list that only the rep builds.

**A strong call** asks which named customers fit and which they have already raised it with.

## 4. Last real activity

**What it is.** The partner names the last real thing they did for the vendor.

**Covered:** the partner names a deal, event or demo and its date. **Partial:** the partner says "recently". **Missed:** no activity is named.

**Does not count:** "we talk to customers all the time". A promise of future activity.

**A strong call** asks for the last time they put the product in front of a customer.

## 5. Enablement status

**What it is.** The partner says who is trained and what is missing.

**Covered:** the partner names who is trained and who is not. **Partial:** the partner says "we are trained" with no names. **Missed:** training does not come up.

**Does not count:** access to a course, with no completion stated.

**A strong call** asks who has finished the training and what blocked the others.

## 6. Dated joint plan

**What it is.** The call ends with a plan that has dates and owners on both sides.

**Covered:** both sides own a step and a date. **Partial:** a plan is agreed with no date or one owner. **Missed:** no plan is agreed.

**Does not count:** "soon". "Let me get the engineer through the course first" with no date.

**A strong call** books the date and the owner for each step before the call ends.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize an audit.

- A call that covered every dimension with the partner's own words must not come back mostly Missed. When it does, widen what counts as evidence.
- A call that never raised a dimension must not get credit for it. When a status rests on a line from a "does not count" list, remove that status.

## Sources

The dimensions follow common channel-management practice. The evidence rules and the strong-call lines come from reviews of recorded partner and sales calls.
