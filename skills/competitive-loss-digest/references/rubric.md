# Competitive-Loss-Digest rules

Reads an export of closed-lost deals. It counts by rival and by reason, and it tags every reason as buyer-stated or rep-stated, because a loss reason that only the rep writes is a guess. With few rows it says so.

Three rules apply to every section.

- **Cite or leave it out.** Every claim traces to a quote, a timestamp, or a column and cell. A claim with no cited source does not ship.
- **The buyer or customer must say it.** A claim that only the rep makes does not count unless the other side accepted it. Silence is not a yes.
- **Unknown stays unknown.** When the input does not say it, write "not stated". Do not fill the gap with a guess or with outside knowledge.

## Scope

State the number of lost rows, how many name a rival, how many have a blank competitor, and which columns were found.

## Losses by rival

For each named rival give the count and the total amount lost. Rank by amount. Blank competitor rows are counted once under "no rival named".

## Reasons by rival

For each rival list the reasons given and the count of each. Mark each reason as buyer-stated or rep-stated. Cite the rows.

## How much is trustworthy

State the share of reasons that the buyer stated. Say plainly that rep-stated reasons are tentative.

## Questions for product and sales

Write up to three questions, each tied to a repeated buyer-stated reason.

## What does not count

- A reason that only the rep states, written as the cause of the loss.
- A pattern from one row.
- A rival with no row in the export.
- A claim about what a rival does that the rows do not state.
- A trend over time from a single export.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize.

- An export with no repeated rival or reason must produce a short digest that says so. Do not rank rivals on one row each.
- A thin input must not get a padded output. When the input supports two items, write two. Do not invent a third to reach a count.

## Sources

The structure follows common win-loss analysis practice. The rules come from reviews of exports and debrief calls.
