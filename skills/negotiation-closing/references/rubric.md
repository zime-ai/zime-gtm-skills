# Negotiation & closing rubric

Six dimensions covering whether a late-stage deal is genuinely moving
toward signature, not just labeled "closing soon."

Three rules apply to every dimension.

- **The buyer must say it.** Evidence is something the buyer said or did. A claim that only the rep makes, such as "they seemed fine with the number", does not count. Silence is not a yes.
- **Absence is a result.** A dimension that the call never touched is Missed. A deal that the rep calls close is not evidence of closing.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each dimension lists the usual false positives.

## 1. Reaction to pricing

**What it is.** How the prospect actually reacted to pricing — pushback,
silence, enthusiasm — taken at face value rather than explained away.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a specific reaction cited and
whether it was resolved on the call. A miss looks like pricing sentiment
going unaddressed or assumed positive with no evidence.

**Weak:** "They seemed fine with the number." **Strong:** "They pushed back
on the annual commitment, we agreed to a quarterly pilot period instead,
and they accepted that on the call."

**Does not count:** worry about price on its own. A discount request that nothing answers. A reaction that the rep assumes from the buyer's tone.

**A strong call** asks for the buyer's reaction in words and resolves each objection before the call ends.

## 2. Procurement process is moving

**What it is.** Whether the deal is actively progressing through the
prospect's procurement steps, versus sitting with no visible movement.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a named procurement step
completed or actively underway. A miss looks like "procurement is looking
at it" with no evidence of actual movement since the last update.

**Weak:** "It's with legal now." **Strong:** "Security review closed
Tuesday, contract is now with their procurement lead who confirmed a
same-week turnaround."

**Does not count:** "it is with legal" with no step named. A step that the rep says is underway while the buyer names no owner or date.

**A strong call** asks who runs each procurement step and what date each step must finish.

## 3. Contracting awareness

**What it is.** Whether the prospect understands what contracting actually
involves on their side — legal review, security questionnaire, signature
authority — rather than discovering it late.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like the prospect naming their own
internal steps unprompted. A miss looks like contracting requirements
surfacing for the first time this call, late in the cycle.

**Weak:** "They know they'll need to sign something." **Strong:** "Their
counsel flagged our DPA needs their standard redlines, which they said
takes about a week internally."

**Does not count:** "they know they will need to sign something". A requirement that the rep lists and the buyer never names.

**A strong call** asks the buyer to list their own internal steps and how long each takes.

## 4. Closure timeline specificity

**What it is.** A specific date or event driving close, not a vague "soon"
that has already moved once.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a stated date tied to a concrete
trigger. A miss looks like "soon" or "this quarter" repeated from an
earlier call with nothing more specific added.

**Weak:** "We're hoping to close this quarter." **Strong:** "They confirmed
signature by the 30th, contingent only on the redline turnaround above."

**Does not count:** "soon" or "this quarter" repeated from an earlier call. A date for a follow-up meeting. A go-live date or a change to a running engagement, which is not a signing date.

**A strong call** asks for the buyer's own signing date, tied to a contract or legal step.

## 5. Budget confirmed at this stage

**What it is.** Budget explicitly confirmed for this specific proposal, not
just assumed plausible from earlier qualification.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like the economic buyer confirming
the actual number on this call. A miss looks like budget carried forward
from an early qualification call with no re-confirmation at this price
point.

**Weak:** "Budget shouldn't be an issue, they mentioned it earlier."
**Strong:** "Their CFO confirmed the $85K figure on this call and said it's
already been allocated."

**Does not count:** budget carried forward from an early call. A figure from the rep's proposal that the buyer has not confirmed.

**A strong call** asks the economic buyer to confirm the number on this call.

## 6. Clear path to signature

**What it is.** A specific next action toward signature — a redline
turnaround, a named approver's sign-off, a signing date — not general
optimism.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a named owner and date for the
final step. A miss looks like the call ending with nothing concrete
calendared toward signature.

**Weak:** "We're close, just a few more steps." **Strong:** "Their VP signs
once legal returns redlines Friday — that's the one thing standing between
here and signature."

**Does not count:** "we will get it done soon". A deal called close while a pricing or legal blocker is still open. A step with no owner and no date.

**A strong call** ends with the next signature step, its owner, and a date.

## Reads-well-too test

Sanity-check against a negotiation call you know led to a fast, clean
close. If it still comes back mostly Missed, loosen the read before
reporting.
