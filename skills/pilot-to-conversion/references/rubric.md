# Pilot-to-conversion rubric

Eight dimensions covering whether a pilot or POC is on track to convert,
not just running.

Three rules apply to every dimension.

- **The buyer must say it.** Evidence is something the buyer or the pilot users said, or a figure from their own data that they accept on the call. A claim that only the rep makes, such as "they love it", does not count. Early pilot feedback is not adoption.
- **Absence is a result.** A dimension that the check-in never touched is Missed. Do not credit it because the tone was friendly.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each dimension lists the usual false positives.

## 1. Plan & success criteria agreed upfront

**What it is.** A specific, mutually agreed plan for what the pilot would
test and what "success" means, established before it started.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like both sides referencing a
specific metric or use case agreed at kickoff. A miss looks like the pilot
having begun without ever naming a finish line.

**Weak:** "We're just seeing how it goes." **Strong:** "We agreed at
kickoff that success means processing 500 claims a day with under a 2%
error rate."

**Does not count:** "we are just seeing how it goes". A success list that the rep wrote and the buyer did not accept. Benchmarks that the vendor set.

**A strong check-in** asks the buyer for pass and fail thresholds and the scenarios that must pass.

## 2. Technical integration readiness

**What it is.** Whether the pilot is actually integrated with the systems
it needs to touch, in a way that reflects real production conditions.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a named integration ("connected
to their Salesforce sandbox") confirmed working. A miss looks like the
pilot running on sample data or a standalone environment with no mention of
how it connects to anything real.

**Weak:** "The integration is mostly there." **Strong:** "The API
connection to their ticketing system went live last week and is pulling
real tickets now."

**Does not count:** "the integration is mostly there". A pilot that runs on sample data and is called integrated.

**A strong check-in** names each connected system and says whether it runs on real data.

## 3. Admin & security readiness

**What it is.** Access, permissions, and any required security review are
sorted, not stalling the pilot on internal process.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like "all users have SSO access
configured." A miss looks like the call surfacing that half the intended
users still can't log in.

**Weak:** "IT is looking into access." **Strong:** "Security review closed
last Tuesday, and all 12 pilot users have been provisioned."

**Does not count:** "IT is looking into access" with no name and no date. An open security or legal approval that nobody owns.

**A strong check-in** names who owns each open approval and when it must close.

## 4. Setup experience so far

**What it is.** Whether the people running the pilot got a genuinely smooth
start, versus still fighting basic setup partway through.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a specific positive or negative
account of onboarding. A miss looks like setup going unmentioned entirely
this far into the pilot.

**Weak:** "Setup's been fine." **Strong:** "Onboarding took half a day
instead of the week they budgeted — their admin said it was the smoothest
tool rollout they've done."

**Does not count:** "setup has been fine". A smooth start that only the rep reports.

**A strong check-in** asks the admin how setup went, compared with what they planned.

## 5. User attendance & engagement

**What it is.** Whether the intended users are actually using the pilot
regularly, since fading usage mid-pilot is one of the strongest early
signals a conversion won't happen.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a specific usage figure or named
active users. A miss looks like engagement going unaddressed or a vague
"people are using it."

**Weak:** "People seem to be logging in." **Strong:** "8 of the 10 seats
logged in this week versus all 10 in week one — usage is dropping and we
haven't asked why yet."

**Does not count:** "people are logging in". A usage claim with no figure.

**A strong check-in** brings a seat count against the first week and asks about any drop.

## 6. Feature-level feedback captured

**What it is.** Specific feedback on what's working and what isn't, rather
than the check-in staying at a vague "going fine" level.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a named feature and a specific
reaction to it. A miss looks like "everyone's happy with it" with no
feature ever named.

**Weak:** "Feedback's been positive overall." **Strong:** "The bulk-export
feature got flagged twice as too slow; the dashboard view got called out by
name as the best part."

**Does not count:** "feedback has been positive overall". Praise from a general user with no feature named.

**A strong check-in** asks which feature helped and which one got in the way.

## 7. Results measured against criteria

**What it is.** Actual evidence — a number, an observed outcome — compared
against the criteria set at kickoff.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a result cited against the
specific metric named in dimension 1. A miss looks like "success" still
undefined or unmeasured this far in.

**Weak:** "It's trending in the right direction." **Strong:** "We're at a
1.8% error rate against the 2% target, three weeks into the pilot."

**Does not count:** "it looks good" with no criterion and no number. Results that only the rep reports. A result against a benchmark that the vendor set.

**A strong check-in** reports each result against the buyer's own baseline and the agreed threshold.

## 8. Clear conversion path

**What it is.** A specific next step toward a decision — a defined end
date, a stakeholder review, a proposal — produced by the call.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a named date and owner for the
next decision point. A miss looks like the pilot left open-ended with no
path to closing.

**Weak:** "We'll keep evaluating." **Strong:** "Their VP will review
results against the agreed criteria on the 20th and decide on conversion
that week."

**Does not count:** "we will follow up after the pilot". A decision date that the rep proposes and the buyer never accepts. Silence from the sponsor.

**A strong check-in** names who decides at the end, by what date, and what result means go.

## Reads-well-too test

Sanity-check against a pilot you know converted well. If it still comes
back mostly Missed, loosen the read before reporting.
