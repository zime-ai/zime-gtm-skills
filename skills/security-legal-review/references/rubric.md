# Security-and-Legal-Review rubric

This rubric has seven dimensions. They show whether the security and legal steps of a deal are mapped before they stall it.

Three rules apply to every dimension.

- **The buyer must say it.** Evidence is something the buyer said. A requirement or a date that only the rep states does not count. A claim that only the rep makes does not count.
- **Absence is a result.** A dimension that the call never touched is Missed. Do not credit it because the call felt friendly.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each dimension lists the usual false positives.


## 1. Requirements known up front

**What it is.** The buyer names the specific security and legal requirements before the rep sends anything.

**Covered:** the buyer names the specific requirements, such as a certification, data-processing terms, or hosting rules. **Partial:** the buyer says "the usual security stuff". **Missed:** no requirement is named.

**Does not count:** a standard list that the rep reads out and the buyer does not confirm. "We will see what they need".

**A strong call** asks for the buyer's list of requirements before sending a document.

## 2. Reviewers named

**What it is.** The buyer names who runs the security review and the legal review.

**Covered:** the buyer names a person and a role for each review. **Partial:** the buyer names a team and no person. **Missed:** nobody is named.

**Does not count:** "IT will handle it". A job title with no stated role in the review.

**A strong call** asks who runs each step and whether that person can join a call.

## 3. Document status

**What it is.** The questionnaire, report, or contract has a status with a sent date and a due date.

**Covered:** a named document has a sent date and an agreed due date. **Partial:** the document is sent or promised with no due date. **Missed:** no document status is discussed.

**Does not count:** "we will send it over". A due date that only the rep proposes.

**A strong call** books the return date before the call ends.

## 4. Steps and durations

**What it is.** The buyer gives each step's order and how long it took for the last vendor.

**Covered:** the buyer gives the steps in order and a duration for each. **Partial:** the buyer gives some steps or some durations. **Missed:** no steps or durations come up.

**Does not count:** a hypothetical such as "how long does review usually take?". A duration that only the rep guesses.

**A strong call** asks for the previous vendor's timeline step by step.

## 5. Fit with the decision date

**What it is.** The steps are tested against the buyer's decision date.

**Covered:** the rep adds up the steps against the date and says whether the date is realistic. **Partial:** the date and the steps both appear and nobody tests them together. **Missed:** neither is discussed, or the rep accepts the date with no test.

**Does not count:** a date for the next meeting. "That should work" with no arithmetic.

**A strong call** works backward from the decision date through each step.

## 6. Blockers sorted fatal versus minor

**What it is.** Open concerns are named, and the buyer says which ones can stop signature.

**Covered:** the buyer states open concerns and which ones block signing. **Partial:** a concern appears with no word on whether it blocks. **Missed:** no concern is raised or asked about.

**Does not count:** a minor complaint called a blocker. A concern that only the rep names.

**A strong call** asks what stops signature and what only slows it.

## 7. Owners and dates for open items

**What it is.** Every open item has an owner and a due date that the buyer accepted.

**Covered:** each open item has an owner and a due date, and the buyer agrees. **Partial:** some items have an owner or a date. **Missed:** the call ends with open items and no owner or date.

**Does not count:** "I will look into it" with no owner or date. A date that the buyer never accepts.

**A strong call** ends with a dated list and reads it back.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize an audit.

- A call that mapped the requirements, the reviewers, the steps, and the dates must not come back mostly Missed. When it does, widen what counts as evidence.
- A call that never raised a dimension must not get credit for it. When a status rests on a line from a "does not count" list, remove that status.

## Sources

The dimensions follow common enterprise procurement practice. The evidence rules and the strong-call lines come from reviews of recorded sales calls.
