# Customer success / QBR rubric

Eight dimensions for a periodic health review — a broader lens than a
risk-triggered or opportunity-triggered conversation. The point is an
honest snapshot of the relationship, not a status-update formality.

Three rules apply to every dimension.

- **The customer must say it.** Evidence is something the customer said, or a figure from their own data that they accept on the call. A claim that only the rep makes, such as "they love it", does not count. Early pilot praise is not adoption.
- **Absence is a result.** A dimension that the review never touched is Missed. Do not credit it because the tone was warm.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each dimension lists the usual false positives.

## 1. Value delivered against original goals

**What it is.** Whether the customer connects current usage back to what
they originally set out to achieve, in specific terms.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a specific original goal
referenced and tied to a current result. A miss looks like the original
goal forgotten and the conversation drifting into generic status updates.

**Weak:** "Things are going well overall." **Strong:** "We set out to cut
onboarding time in half, and we're now at a 45% reduction six months in."

**Does not count:** "things are going well". A goal that the rep restates and the customer does not recognize as theirs.

**A strong call** asks which original goal has moved most and what the customer measures it with.

## 2. Usage breadth across the account

**What it is.** Whether usage is spread across the team or account, versus
concentrated in one or two people regardless of how positive the tone is.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a specific count or breakdown of
active users. A miss looks like usage breadth never addressed.

**Weak:** "People are using it." **Strong:** "12 of 15 licensed seats
logged in this month, up from 8 last quarter."

**Does not count:** "people are using it". More use inside what the customer already pays for, which is usage and not expansion. A count with no baseline.

**A strong call** brings a usage figure and labels it healthy, under-used, or over-used, then names a next step.

## 3. Open issues resolved, not just raised

**What it is.** Whether an issue raised in a prior review was actually
closed out, versus raised again with no update.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a prior issue explicitly
referenced with a resolution status. A miss looks like a recurring issue
mentioned with no update on what happened to it last time.

**Weak:** "There's still a small issue with exports." **Strong:** "The
export-formatting issue from last quarter's review is now fixed — they
confirmed it on this call."

**Does not count:** an issue mentioned again with no status. A product issue with no owner and no date.

**A strong call** names the open issue from the last review and says who owns it and when it closes.

## 4. Enablement gaps

**What it is.** Whether there's a training or adoption gap holding back
value, separate from whether the product itself fits the need.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a specific gap named. A miss
looks like underuse observed with no explanation offered.

**Weak:** "They could use more training maybe." **Strong:** "Their new
hires never got onboarded to the reporting module — that's why usage
dipped after the team grew."

**Does not count:** "they could use more training maybe". A gap the rep guesses with no sign from the customer.

**A strong call** names the team or the new hires who missed onboarding and what they need.

## 5. Stakeholder breadth

**What it is.** Whether the conversation is still with the same single
contact from the original deal, or has broadened to other stakeholders.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like multiple named stakeholders on
or referenced in the call. A miss looks like a single point of contact with
no mention of anyone else — a standing risk even absent any other bad
signal.

**Weak:** "Same contact as always, going fine." **Strong:** "This QBR
included their ops lead for the first time, in addition to our original
champion."

**Does not count:** the same single contact with a friendly tone. A stakeholder named by the rep and never on a call.

**A strong call** asks who else sees the results and invites them to the next review.

## 6. Strategic alignment

**What it is.** Whether the account connects to something the customer's
leadership actively cares about this year, versus having become "a tool we
have" with no strategic story.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a named executive priority tied
to the account. A miss looks like no leadership-level connection mentioned.

**Weak:** "It's useful for the team." **Strong:** "Their CFO name-checked
this tool in the board deck as part of the efficiency initiative."

**Does not count:** "it is useful for the team". A priority that the rep names for the customer's leadership.

**A strong call** asks which leadership goal this account supports this year.

## 7. Forward-looking fit

**What it is.** Whether the conversation surfaced anything relevant to
what's next for the account — a new use case, a roadmap need — without it
being a sales push.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a specific future need named by
the customer. A miss looks like the conversation staying entirely
retrospective.

**Weak:** "We might explore more later." **Strong:** "They mentioned
expanding into their EU office next year and asked whether the product
supports multi-region data residency."

**Does not count:** a general wish for "more features". A feature request with no stated link to a goal. Expansion talk that the rep starts and the customer does not follow.

**A strong call** asks what the customer will need next and whether they want a response by a date.

## 8. Clear cadence for next check-in

**What it is.** Whether the call ended with an agreed date or trigger for
the next review, versus drifting until something goes wrong.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a specific date or trigger
agreed on the call. A miss looks like the call ending with no next-review
plan.

**Weak:** "We'll touch base again at some point." **Strong:** "Next QBR
scheduled for the second week of Q2, or sooner if the EU expansion moves
forward."

**Does not count:** "let us keep in touch". A date that the rep proposes and the customer never accepts.

**A strong call** ends with the next review's date, attendees, and agenda, and logs each open promise with an owner and a date.

## Reads-well-too test

Sanity-check against a QBR you know went well. If it still comes back
mostly Missed, loosen the read before reporting.
