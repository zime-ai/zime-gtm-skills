# Pain-finder rubric (generic taxonomy)

Used when no vertical is named. A vertical pack in
`skills/vertical-context/references/` may supply a specialized variant of
these five buckets — see that skill for the contract governing how packs may
and may not change this.

## Five generic buckets

1. **Operational friction** — manual work, broken workflows, tooling gaps,
   things that cost time or introduce error today.
2. **Risk exposure** — what could go wrong, has gone wrong, or nearly went
   wrong if the current state continues.
3. **Cost / headcount pressure** — budget constraints, understaffing,
   inability to get resources approved.
4. **Growth / scale pressure** — the current approach worked at a smaller
   size and is visibly not working now.
5. **Leadership / stakeholder pressure** — a board, executive, or external
   party (auditor, regulator, customer) is pushing for change from above.

A pain point can span more than one bucket — say so rather than forcing a
single tag.

## Confidence calibration

- **High** — described with a specific incident, number, or repeated
  emphasis; the prospect returned to it unprompted.
- **Medium** — clearly stated once, with some specificity, but not dwelt on.
- **Low** — raised only when asked directly, or explicitly downplayed by the
  prospect in the same breath ("that's not really what's keeping me up at
  night"). Report Low pains — don't drop them — but rank them last and never
  let the closing next-step lead with one.

## Hard rules

- Every pain point needs a direct quote. No quote, no pain point — an
  inferred read belongs in Notes, explicitly labeled as inferred.
- If the prospect explicitly downplays something, the write-up must reflect
  that downplaying, not silently upgrade it because it fits a compelling
  category.
- Don't invent a "next steps for your follow-up" section unless asked. This
  skill's job stops at ranking pain, not prescribing a sales motion.
