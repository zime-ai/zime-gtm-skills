# Champion tracker rubric

## Champion definition (reused verbatim from `meddicc`)

> Someone inside the account who will sell on the rep's behalf when the
> rep isn't in the room — actively, not just someone who likes the
> product. Look for evidence of the champion doing something (looping in
> a stakeholder, pushing internally), not just saying positive things.

Source: `skills/meddicc/references/rubric.md`, Champion section. Don't
redefine it here — this skill applies the same bar per-person and across
calls instead of as one letter in a bigger scorecard.

## Action vs Sentiment — the test

**Action.** The contact describes doing something for the deal that
happened, or is happening, outside the current call — something the rep
could not have seen or caused. Evidence it clears the bar:

- Looping in another stakeholder ("I brought Priya from procurement into
  this last week").
- Pushing internally, including in a meeting the rep wasn't in ("I
  pushed back when our CFO raised the budget concern in Monday's
  leadership sync").
- Doing legwork on the rep's behalf (drafting an internal business case,
  scheduling the next internal review, chasing a signature).
- Removing a blocker without being asked.

A future promise ("I'll loop in procurement next week") is **not** yet
Action — it's a plan, tag it Sentiment until a later call (or a later
line in the same call) confirms it happened. If it's confirmed later,
that later instance is a fresh Action row; don't retroactively upgrade
the promise.

**Sentiment.** The contact says something positive or encouraging about
the product, the rep, or the deal, with no accompanying action —
enthusiasm, praise, agreement, excitement. However strong the language,
if nothing was *done*, it's Sentiment:

- "This is exactly what we need."
- "I'm really impressed with the demo."
- "Yeah, we're excited to move forward."
- "Everyone on our side loves this."

Volume or repetition of Sentiment lines does not convert them to Action,
and does not average up to a champion. Ten enthusiastic quotes from the
same contact, with zero cited actions, is still zero Action rows for
that contact.

**Not a row at all.** Small talk, scheduling logistics, and factual
answers to the rep's discovery questions (budget numbers, headcount,
timeline) are neither Action nor Sentiment — they don't belong in the
ledger. This overlaps with, but is distinct from, `next-step-commitment`'s
commitment-tracking, which is about future dates/deliverables rather than
champion behavior specifically.

## Trend read

Compare the champion candidate's Action rows call-over-call, oldest to
newest:

- **Strengthening** — later call(s) show more Action rows, or actions of
  larger scope (e.g. moved from "mentioned it to a colleague" to "got
  procurement formally looped in"), than earlier call(s).
- **Flat** — Action rows present at a similar rate and scope across
  calls, no clear direction.
- **Weakening** — a contact who had Action rows in an earlier call has
  only Sentiment (or nothing) in a later call, or the actions shrink in
  scope.
- **Insufficient history** — only one call was given. Do not infer a
  trend, and do not hedge toward one direction "just in case."

If two different contacts are competing champion candidates, run the
trend read for the strongest candidate as of the latest call, and note
if leadership shifted between calls (e.g. Contact A had the only Action
row in call 1, Contact B took over in call 2).

## The "no champion, only a friend" finding

If, across every named contact and every call given, every single row is
tagged Sentiment, the deal has no champion by this bar — it has a
friendly contact. State this as the headline finding, in those or
similar plain words. This is not a softer version of "strong stakeholder
engagement" — a room full of people who say nice things and do nothing is
a legitimate risk signal (nobody will sell the deal when the rep isn't in
the room), and softening it into generic positive language defeats the
purpose of this skill.

## Reads-well-too check

Don't force a champion onto the ledger to make the deal look healthier
than the evidence supports, and don't withhold a real champion call just
because the deal is otherwise thin. The ledger's job is to reflect what
was actually said and done, not to editorialize the deal's health beyond
that.
