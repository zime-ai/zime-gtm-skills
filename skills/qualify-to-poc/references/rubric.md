# Qualify-to-POC rubric

Five dimensions covering whether a qualified deal is actually ready to
start a POC/pilot — the go/no-go gate before pilot work begins, not a
read on how the pilot itself is going.

## 1. Success criteria defined and agreed

**What it is.** Whether the objective conditions under which the POC counts
as successful are explicit and both sides signed off on them — not just
what the rep hopes to prove, but a decision gate the prospect has actually
agreed to.

**Covered looks like:** specific metrics or thresholds, tied to must-pass
use cases, stated and confirmed by the prospect in their own words.
**Partial looks like:** some criteria named but thresholds or scope stay
high-level ("it should work well"). **Missed looks like:** success is
never defined, or only the rep states what success would look like without
prospect confirmation.

"It should save us time" is Partial; "success means the sync completes
within five minutes with zero manual reconciliation, validated across both
warehouses" is Covered.

## 2. Technical scope agreed

**What it is.** Whether it's clear what's in scope and out of scope for
the POC — which systems it integrates with, which use cases it covers,
which it doesn't — or the scope is fuzzy enough that it could expand or
drift once the pilot starts.

**Covered looks like:** named systems, environments, and use cases, with
an explicit statement of what's excluded. **Partial looks like:** scope
named for one system or use case with no mention of adjacent ones that
could creep in. **Missed looks like:** no scope discussion at all, or "we'll
just see how it goes."

## 3. Stakeholders identified and committed

**What it is.** Whether it's clear who on the customer side will actually
evaluate the pilot day to day, and who has the authority to sign off on the
result at the end.

**Covered looks like:** a named day-to-day evaluator and a named
end-of-pilot decision-maker, both confirmed as committed. **Partial looks
like:** one of the two roles is named but the other is vague ("someone from
IT will look at it"). **Missed looks like:** no named owner or
decision-maker at all — the deal still lacks anyone accountable for the
pilot's outcome.

## 4. POC timeline and exit criteria set

**What it is.** Whether there are agreed start and end dates, and a clear
definition of what "pass" looks like at the end — not an open-ended pilot
with no fixed point where it gets judged.

**Covered looks like:** specific start/end dates and a stated pass/fail
review point. **Partial looks like:** a rough duration ("a few weeks") with
no fixed end date or review moment. **Missed looks like:** no timeline
discussed, or the pilot is framed as open-ended.

"We'll run it for a while and see" is Missed; "we start Monday, and we
review results against the criteria on the 21st" is Covered.

## 5. Resourcing confirmed

**What it is.** Whether a specific named person (or people) on the customer
side will actually do the work the pilot requires — configuration, testing,
feedback — rather than that work being assumed to happen without anyone
committed to it.

**Covered looks like:** a named person with confirmed bandwidth for the
specific work the pilot needs. **Partial looks like:** a team or role named
generically ("our IT team will handle it") without a specific person or
bandwidth confirmation. **Missed looks like:** no one named, or the
customer-side work is assumed without any discussion of who does it.

## Reads-well-too check

Sanity-check against a POC kickoff you know was well-run. If it still
comes back mostly Missed, loosen the read before reporting.
