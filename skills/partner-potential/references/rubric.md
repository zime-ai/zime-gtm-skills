# Partner-Potential rubric

This rubric has 6 dimensions. It audits a call with a channel or reseller partner. The partner is the other side of the call, so partner words count the way buyer words count in a sales call.

Three rules apply to every dimension.

- **The partner must say it.** Evidence is something the partner said. A claim that only the vendor's rep makes, such as "they love us", does not count. Partner size and reputation are not evidence of fit. A claim that only the rep makes does not count.
- **Absence is a result.** A dimension that the call never touched is Missed. Do not credit it because the call felt friendly.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each dimension lists the usual false positives.


## 1. Named end customer

**What it is.** The partner names the customer and who they spoke to.

**Covered:** the partner names the company and a contact with a role. **Partial:** the partner names the company with no contact. **Missed:** no customer is named.

**Does not count:** "a regional chain" with no name. Pipeline talk with no customer.

**A strong call** asks for the company, the contact and the role.

## 2. Fit stated by the partner and the customer

**What it is.** Both the partner and the customer say that the offer fits the customer's problem.

**Covered:** the partner states the problem and the customer's own words or reaction confirm the fit. **Partial:** the partner says it looks useful and the customer's view is relayed. **Missed:** no fit is stated.

**Does not count:** A demo, which is exposure and not fit. A fit that only the rep claims.

**A strong call** asks what the customer said fits, in the customer's words.

## 3. Purchase or registration step

**What it is.** A step toward purchase or registration is named.

**Covered:** a named step with a date, such as a registration filed or a contract step. **Partial:** a step is named with no date, or "we want a pilot". **Missed:** no step is named.

**Does not count:** a pilot or POC alone. "We will register it this week" with no date.

**A strong call** asks for the registration date and the next purchase step.

## 4. Timeline

**What it is.** The partner or customer states when the customer would decide.

**Covered:** a date or a trigger tied to a reason. **Partial:** a rough window with no reason. **Missed:** no timeline comes up.

**Does not count:** "soon". A date for the next meeting.

**A strong call** asks what drives the customer's decision date.

## 5. Who sells and who owns the relationship

**What it is.** The roles in the deal are agreed.

**Covered:** the partner and the vendor agree who leads, who owns the relationship and who supports. **Partial:** one side states the roles and the other does not agree. **Missed:** roles do not come up.

**Does not count:** "probably us". A role that only the rep assigns.

**A strong call** asks who leads each step and gets the other side's agreement.

## 6. Commercial terms known

**What it is.** The margin or discount and the deal type are known.

**Covered:** the terms and the arrangement type are stated. **Partial:** the partner says terms exist and gives none. **Missed:** terms do not come up.

**Does not count:** "we can talk about it later". Terms that only the rep states.

**A strong call** asks the margin tier and the arrangement type, referral or resell.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize an audit.

- A call that covered every dimension with the partner's own words must not come back mostly Missed. When it does, widen what counts as evidence.
- A call that never raised a dimension must not get credit for it. When a status rests on a line from a "does not count" list, remove that status.

## Sources

The dimensions follow common channel-management practice. The evidence rules and the strong-call lines come from reviews of recorded partner and sales calls.
