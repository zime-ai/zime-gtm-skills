# Partner-Decision-Authority rubric

This rubric has 6 dimensions. It audits a call with a channel or reseller partner. The partner is the other side of the call, so partner words count the way buyer words count in a sales call.

Three rules apply to every dimension.

- **The partner must say it.** Evidence is something the partner said. A claim that only the vendor's rep makes, such as "they love us", does not count. Partner size and reputation are not evidence of fit. A claim that only the rep makes does not count.
- **Absence is a result.** A dimension that the call never touched is Missed. Do not credit it because the call felt friendly.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each dimension lists the usual false positives.


## 1. Decider named

**What it is.** The partner names who decides and the role of the person on the call.

**Covered:** the partner names the decider and says the contact's own role, such as recommender. **Partial:** the partner says "leadership" or "the owner" with no name. **Missed:** nobody learns who decides.

**Does not count:** a job title alone. The contact who recommends, treated as the decider.

**A strong call** asks who has the final word and what the contact's role is.

## 2. Decision process

**What it is.** The partner describes the steps and the date of the decision.

**Covered:** the partner states the steps in order and a date or meeting. **Partial:** the partner states some steps. **Missed:** no process comes up.

**Does not count:** "we will think about it". A process that only the rep proposes.

**A strong call** asks for each step and the date of the decision meeting.

## 3. What the decider looks at

**What it is.** The partner states what the decider cares about.

**Covered:** the partner names the criteria, such as margin or hiring needs. **Partial:** the partner names one loose preference. **Missed:** no criteria come up.

**Does not count:** criteria that the rep lists. A reaction to a demo.

**A strong call** asks what the decider looks at first and what would stop a yes.

## 4. Commitment the partner will make

**What it is.** The partner says what they will do, such as write a case or assign a person.

**Covered:** the partner names a step they will take and a date. **Partial:** the partner says they will help. **Missed:** no commitment comes up.

**Does not count:** "let me know how it goes". A step that only the rep owns.

**A strong call** asks what the contact will do before the decider meets and by when.

## 5. What competes for attention

**What it is.** The partner names the other priorities that compete with this vendor.

**Covered:** the partner names a competing priority and when it lands. **Partial:** the partner says they are busy. **Missed:** no competing priority comes up.

**Does not count:** "we are busy". A priority that only the rep guesses.

**A strong call** asks what else the decider is deciding this quarter.

## 6. Dated decision

**What it is.** A date for the decision is stated.

**Covered:** the partner states a date and the meeting. **Partial:** the partner states a rough window. **Missed:** no date comes up.

**Does not count:** "soon". A date for the next call.

**A strong call** asks the date of the decision and who will tell the vendor.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize an audit.

- A call that covered every dimension with the partner's own words must not come back mostly Missed. When it does, widen what counts as evidence.
- A call that never raised a dimension must not get credit for it. When a status rests on a line from a "does not count" list, remove that status.

## Sources

The dimensions follow common channel-management practice. The evidence rules and the strong-call lines come from reviews of recorded partner and sales calls.
