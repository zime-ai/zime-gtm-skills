# Demo-call rubric

This rubric has seven dimensions. They show whether a demo was tailored, well attended, and ended with a clear next step. It is not a checklist of features shown.

Three rules apply to every dimension.

- **Quote the right speaker.** For what the rep did, quote the rep. For what the buyer wants, thinks, or reacted to, the buyer must say it. A claim from the rep that the buyer is happy does not count.
- **Absence is a result.** A dimension that the call never touched is Missed. Do not credit a dimension because the demo went smoothly.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each dimension lists the usual false positives. Read that line before you write Covered or Partial.

A call that only has slides or spoken descriptions has no live walk-through. Say so in one line and score the dimensions that remain.

## 1. Tailored to stated priorities

**What it is.** The demo walked through what this buyer said mattered on earlier calls. The weak case is a generic tour of the product, whatever came up in discovery.

**Covered:** the rep opens by restating the buyer's problem in the buyer's words and demos against it directly. **Partial:** the priority gets addressed, but only after a generic walkthrough or only briefly. **Missed:** the demo never refers to anything specific to this buyer. It looks the same for any account.

**Does not count:** the rep naming the buyer's company or industry once. A feature list that the rep chose.

**A strong call** shows only the steps that solve the stated problem and skips the rest.

## 2. Right stakeholders present

**What it is.** The people who must be convinced were in the room. They can be technical evaluators, the economic buyer, or end users. The weak case is the same one or two people from earlier calls.

**Covered:** a named economic buyer or technical evaluator who was not on an earlier call attends and engages. **Partial:** a new stakeholder attends but says little, or their role is unclear. **Missed:** the attendee list matches discovery and nobody tried to widen it.

**Does not count:** an attendee who only listens and whose role nobody states. A job title with no stated role in the decision.

**A strong call** asks before the demo who else must see it and invites them.

## 3. Engagement during the demo

**What it is.** The buyer asked questions, reacted to specific features, or pushed back. The weak case is a buyer who sat silent. Silence is a weaker signal than it looks, so judge the specificity of the reaction and not the volume of talk.

**Covered:** the buyer asks a specific question about a feature or raises a concrete objection. **Partial:** the buyer responds after a prompt and never starts a topic. **Missed:** no reaction beyond "okay" and "got it" for the whole call.

**Does not count:** polite nods. "This is useful" with no feature named. A feasibility question such as "can it do X?" with no follow-up, which shows curiosity and not interest.

**A strong call** pauses after each step and asks what the buyer wants to change.

## 4. Competitive differentiation addressed

**What it is.** The buyer is evaluating alternatives, and the demo addressed why this product is different or better. The weak case is a product shown in a vacuum.

**Covered:** the rep names a specific alternative, either a competitor or the status quo, and demos the specific difference. **Partial:** the rep implies a difference with no named alternative. **Missed:** competition never comes up although the buyer is known to evaluate options.

**Does not count:** general praise of the product. A comparison that the rep makes and the buyer does not engage with.

**A strong call** states plainly what the product does not do yet, with an owner and a date.

## 5. Fit against current tooling

**What it is.** The demo connected to what the buyer uses today: what it replaces and what it works with. The weak case is a demo that floats apart from the buyer's environment.

**Covered:** the rep names the buyer's current tool or process and shows the specific handoff or integration point. The rep says which integrations exist today and which are only planned. **Partial:** the rep refers to current tooling in general terms ("your existing system") with no specifics. **Missed:** the demo never engages with what the buyer uses today.

**Does not count:** an integration that the rep describes without saying whether it exists now. A tool that the rep assumes the buyer uses.

**A strong call** runs one example on data of the kind the buyer holds, from start to finish.

## 6. Success criteria reconfirmed

**What it is.** The demo returned to the outcome that the buyer said they want and showed it. The weak case is a list of capabilities and hope that they land.

**Covered:** the rep states the buyer's earlier success criterion and demos that outcome. **Partial:** the rep gestures at a general outcome with no tie to the stated criterion. **Missed:** no return to any goal that the buyer stated before.

**Does not count:** "this saves time" with no number. A criterion that the rep invents.

**A strong call** closes by asking whether the demo met the stated criterion and what is still open.

## 7. Clear outcome and next step

**What it is.** The call ended with a specific next step that both sides agreed to. The weak case is a vague "we will follow up".

**Covered:** a specific next step with an owner and a date or timeframe. **Partial:** a next step is named with no owner or no timeframe. **Missed:** "we will follow up", or the call ends with no agreed action.

**Does not count:** "let us reconnect sometime". A promise by the rep to look into a gap, with no owner and no due date.

**A strong call** ends with a proposed day, the people who must attend, and an agenda. It also logs each open promise with an owner and a date.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize an audit.

- A demo that you know landed well must not come back mostly Missed. When it does, widen what counts as evidence.
- A demo that never touched a dimension must not get credit for it. When a status rests on a line from a "does not count" list, remove that status.

## Sources

The dimensions follow common demo-coaching practice. The evidence rules and the "strong call" lines come from reviews of recorded sales calls.
