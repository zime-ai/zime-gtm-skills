# Demo-call rubric

Seven dimensions covering whether a demo was tailored, well-attended, and
landed with a clear next step — not a checklist of features shown.

## 1. Tailored to stated priorities

**What it is.** Whether the demo walked through what this specific
prospect said mattered on earlier calls, or was a generic tour of the
product regardless of what came up in discovery.

**Covered:** the rep opens by naming the prospect's stated priority and
demos against it directly. **Partial:** the priority gets addressed, but
only after a generic walkthrough, or only briefly. **Missed:** the demo
never references anything specific to this prospect — it would look
identical for any account.

## 2. Right stakeholders present

**What it is.** Whether the people who actually need to be convinced were
in the room — technical evaluators, economic buyer, end users — or just
the same one or two people from earlier calls.

**Covered:** a named economic buyer or technical evaluator not previously
on a call attends and engages. **Partial:** a new stakeholder attends but
says little, or their role/influence is unclear. **Missed:** the attendee
list is unchanged from discovery, with no attempt to expand it.

## 3. Engagement during the demo

**What it is.** Whether the prospect asked questions, reacted to specific
features, or pushed back on anything — or sat largely silent through the
whole thing. Silence during a demo is a weaker signal than it looks; judge
by specificity of reaction, not volume of talking.

**Covered:** the prospect asks a specific, non-generic question about a
feature or raises a concrete objection. **Partial:** the prospect responds
when prompted but never initiates. **Missed:** no reaction beyond
perfunctory acknowledgment ("okay," "got it") for the entire call.

## 4. Competitive differentiation addressed

**What it is.** If the prospect is evaluating alternatives, whether the
demo addressed why this is different or better, or just showed the product
in a vacuum.

**Covered:** the rep names a specific alternative (competitor or status
quo) and demos the specific difference. **Partial:** differentiation is
implied but not tied to a named alternative. **Missed:** competition is
never mentioned even though the prospect is known to be evaluating options.

## 5. Fit against current tooling

**What it is.** Whether the demo connected back to what the prospect uses
today — what it replaces, what it integrates with — or floated
disconnected from their actual environment.

**Covered:** the rep names the prospect's current tool/process by name and
shows the specific handoff or integration point. **Partial:** current
tooling is referenced generically ("your existing system") without
specifics. **Missed:** the demo never engages with what the prospect
actually uses today.

## 6. Success criteria reconfirmed

**What it is.** Whether the demo circled back to the outcome the prospect
said they wanted, and showed it specifically — or just showed capabilities
and hoped they'd land.

**Covered:** the rep states the prospect's previously-stated success
criterion and demos exactly that outcome. **Partial:** a general outcome is
gestured at without being tied to the specific criterion. **Missed:** no
callback to any previously stated goal.

## 7. Clear outcome and next step

**What it is.** Whether the call ended with a specific next step both sides
agreed to, or a vague "we'll follow up."

**Covered:** a specific next step with an owner and a date/timeframe.
**Partial:** a next step is named but no owner or timeframe. **Missed:**
"we'll follow up" or the call ends with no agreed action at all.

## Reads-well-too check

Sanity-check against a demo you know landed well. If it still comes back
mostly Missed, loosen the read before reporting.
