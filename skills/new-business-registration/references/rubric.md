# New-business registration rubric

Six dimensions covering whether a new-logo deal registration is complete
enough to trust, plus a reads-well-too check. Deal registration has no
canonical public framework the way MEDDICC or BANT does — this rubric is
built from general channel/deal-registration practice (duplicate/conflict
checking, source attribution, ICP fit), not a named standard.

## 1. Legal entity + domain identified

**What it is.** The registration is tied to a specific legal entity and
domain, not just a company name that could match several entities (parent,
subsidiary, DBA).

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a full legal name and domain
recorded on the registration. A miss looks like a bare company name that
could belong to any of several related entities.

**Weak:** "Acme" as the entire entity field. **Strong:** "Acme
Manufacturing LLC, domain acmemfg.com — confirmed distinct from Acme
Holdings, the parent."

## 2. Duplicate/conflict check run

**What it is.** The account or pipeline was checked for an existing owner
or open opportunity before registering.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a stated check result, even if
the result is "no conflict found." A miss looks like the record showing no
evidence a check ever happened.

**Weak:** "Should be a new account." **Strong:** "Checked against pipeline
— no existing opportunity or owner found as of today's date."

## 3. Source attribution recorded

**What it is.** How this opportunity was found — inbound, referral,
prospecting, event, partner-sourced — is clearly stated.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a specific, named source. A miss
looks like the source field left blank or vague ("networking").

**Weak:** "Came in somehow through marketing." **Strong:** "Inbound from
the March webinar registration list, filled out the demo request form
directly."

## 4. ICP fit stated, not assumed

**What it is.** A stated reason this account fits the ideal customer
profile (segment, size, use case), not just fit implied by the
registration existing.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a specific fit reason named. A
miss looks like fit never addressed, assumed by default.

**Weak:** "Seems like a good fit." **Strong:** "200-person mid-market SaaS
company, matches our target segment on size and the exact use case
(outbound call coaching) we sell against."

## 5. Requested support/resources specified

**What it is.** What's being asked for — deal registration protection,
technical resources, marketing support — is named on the registration.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a specific ask listed. A miss
looks like the ask left unspecified.

**Weak:** "Just registering it." **Strong:** "Requesting a technical SE for
the first discovery call and standard 90-day deal protection."

## 6. Registration expiry / validity window set

**What it is.** A defined validity window or expiry date on the
registration, not left open-ended.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a specific expiry date recorded.
A miss looks like no review point ever set.

**Weak:** "No end date needed, it's ours." **Strong:** "90-day protection
window from today, reviewed automatically if no stage movement by then."

## Reads-well-too check

Sanity-check against a registration you know was clean and uncontested. If
it still comes back mostly Missed, loosen the read before reporting.
