# FAINT rubric

Five criteria, built for demand-driven leads early in the sales process. FAINT
is designed for prospects who may have no allocated budget and no articulated
problem yet, but who show real financial capacity and genuine curiosity. The
scoring order is deliberate: Interest is judged before Need, and a call can
score well without a stated Need — unlike a strict BANT read.

## Funds

**What it is.** Whether the prospect's organization has genuine financial
capacity to spend on a solution like yours — whether that capacity is today's
cash, a committed budget, or a credible ability to find money if a compelling
case exists. This is looser than BANT's Budget: you're testing for "can they
spend?" not "did they pre-allocate?"

**Covered looks like:** a prospect who states the org is financially healthy
and spending wouldn't require a major ask; confirms money is available, expected
in the current cycle, or could be approved without undue friction. **Partial
looks like:** financial capacity exists but is conditional — requires approval,
or the prospect is vague about availability while not outright declining.
**Missed looks like:** the prospect states the org is cash-constrained, has no
money in this area, or explicitly says spend would be very difficult to justify.

"We'd have to ask finance first" is Partial; "we're doing fine this year, if
this made sense we could absorb it" is Covered.

## Authority

**What it is.** Whether the person on the call has a real path to influencing
or making a purchase decision — not simply "is on the call," and not simply
title. Distinguish between the actual decision-maker, an influencer with a
documented path to one, and someone with no realistic decision path.

**Covered looks like:** the person on the call either is the decision-maker or
explicitly names the decision-maker and describes their own role in moving it
forward. **Partial looks like:** the person references a decision-maker ("my
director owns this") without naming them, or describes their influence vaguely.
**Missed looks like:** no one on the call can name or describe a path to a
decision-maker, or the person's role is clearly unrelated to the purchase.

A prospect saying "I can move this to my VP, she decides" (naming the person)
is Covered; saying "I'd have to check with my director" is Partial.

## Interest

**What it is.** Genuine curiosity the rep drew out of the prospect during the
call — a substantive question, a request for more information, or a reaction
showing they hadn't considered this angle before. Score this on what the rep
generated, not on what the prospect brought in; inbound intent and curiosity
built in the call are different signals.

**Covered looks like:** the prospect explicitly asks a follow-up question,
requests an artifact ("send me a one-pager"), or reacts with genuine surprise
or new thinking. **Partial looks like:** polite listening and engagement without
a substantive question back, or a single question that doesn't go deeper.
**Missed looks like:** the prospect disengages, expresses skepticism, or gives
yes-or-no answers only.

"That's actually interesting — can you send me a one-pager?" is Covered; "yeah,
okay, sounds good" is Partial; "I don't see how this applies to us" is Missed.

## Need

**What it is.** A specific business problem the prospect acknowledged in their
own words — rooted in what they actually do, tied to a business impact, and
recognized as real by the prospect themselves. Score this against what Interest
already established: Interest comes first, and shapes how you read Need.

**Covered looks like:** the prospect describes a concrete problem tied to
business impact or frustration; it's central to the call or mentioned more than
once. **Partial looks like:** a real problem named once, without much detail on
cause or impact, or a problem surfaced but not owned as urgent. **Missed looks
like:** only category-level interest ("we're always looking to improve"), with
no specific problem the prospect names or acknowledges as real.

A prospect saying "we spend too much time on manual re-keying and it slows us
down" is Covered; "we could probably be more efficient" is Partial.

## Timing

**What it is.** Whether the prospect points to a real reason this decision might
move on a specific timeline — a renewal window, a budget cycle, a competing
evaluation, or a business event triggering a change — versus vague timing like
"sometime this year." Vague timing is a leading reason an apparently-interested
lead goes quiet.

**Covered looks like:** the prospect names a specific date, deadline, renewal
window, or an active competing evaluation. **Partial looks like:** a rough
window or trigger mentioned but vague or conditional ("maybe this quarter,"
"when we do our next review"). **Missed looks like:** no timeline surfaced, a
timeline 12+ months out, or explicit statements that timing is not a factor.

"Our license renews in March" is Covered; "maybe around year-end planning" is
Partial; "no real timeline right now" is Missed.

## Interest before Need

The single thing that distinguishes FAINT from BANT: don't let "no stated Need"
pull the audit down if Interest is genuinely Covered. FAINT exists for prospects
who haven't articulated a need yet — that's the premise, not a failure. Only
treat weak Need as a real risk when Interest is also weak. When Interest is
strong, frame Need as "not yet surfaced" and name the call that would surface
it, not as a disqualifying miss.

## Reads-well-too check

A call that clearly built genuine interest and showed financial capacity should
not come back mostly Missed just because no stated Need surfaced. If that
happens, re-score Need using the Interest-before-Need rule above before
finalizing — the audit should reflect whether the call did its job at this
stage of the relationship.
