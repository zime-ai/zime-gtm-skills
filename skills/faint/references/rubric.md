# FAINT rubric

Five criteria. FAINT (Mike Schultz, RAIN Group) was built for demand-driven
B2B sales, where a lead may show up with no allocated budget and no stated
problem, but real financial capacity and enough curiosity to be worth
pursuing. The scoring order below is deliberate: Interest is judged before
Need, and a call can score well overall without a stated Need, the way a
strict BANT read would not allow.

## Funds

General financial capacity to spend on a solution like this, regardless of
whether a specific line item exists yet — looser than BANT's Budget. "We're
doing fine this year, this wouldn't be a stretch" is Covered even with no
number attached; "we'd have to ask finance if this is even possible" is
Partial; a prospect with no plausible ability to spend is Missed.

## Authority

Is the person on the call the decision-maker, an influencer feeding a
decision-maker, or someone with no real path to a decision? Distinguish
these explicitly rather than assuming "in a call = has authority."

## Interest

Genuine curiosity the rep generated on this call — the prospect asking a
follow-up question, requesting a follow-up artifact, or reacting to
something they hadn't considered before. This is the criterion FAINT weighs
most heavily and scores before Need: score it on what the rep drew out of
the prospect, not on inbound intent the prospect arrived with. "That's
actually interesting, can you send me more on that" is Covered; polite
listening with no question back is Partial; a prospect who disengages is
Missed.

## Need

A specific problem the prospect named in their own words. Score this against
what Interest already established: a call that built real curiosity but
hasn't yet surfaced a named problem is still a legitimate FAINT pass — mark
Need Partial rather than Missed, and say so. Reserve Missed for a call where
the rep also failed to generate any interest, since a demand-driven lead can
be worth pursuing on Interest alone.

## Timing

Is there a real reason this could move on a specific timeframe — a
renewal, a budget cycle, a triggering event — or is "sometime down the
line" the most specific thing said? Vague timing is a common reason an
apparently-interested lead goes quiet.

## Interest before Need

The single thing that distinguishes FAINT from BANT: don't let "no stated
Need" pull the whole audit down if Interest is genuinely Covered. FAINT
exists for prospects who haven't articulated a need yet — that's the
premise, not a gap. Only treat weak Need as a real risk when Interest is
also weak; when Interest is strong, frame Need as "not yet surfaced" and
name the next call that would surface it, not as a disqualifying miss.

## Reads-well-too check

A call that clearly built genuine interest and showed financial capacity
should not come back mostly Missed just because it didn't also produce a
stated Need. If that happens, re-score Need using the Interest-before-Need
rule above before finalizing.
