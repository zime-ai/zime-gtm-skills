# Account-Plan-Generator rules

Writes the plan from the calls with one account. It uses nothing about the company from outside the transcripts. A person's role comes from what that person or a colleague said, not from a job title. It does not invent expansion ideas.

Three rules apply to every section.

- **Cite or leave it out.** Every claim traces to a quote, a timestamp, or a column and cell. A claim with no cited source does not ship.
- **The buyer or customer must say it.** A claim that only the rep makes does not count unless the other side accepted it. Silence is not a yes.
- **Unknown stays unknown.** When the input does not say it, write "not stated". Do not fill the gap with a guess or with outside knowledge.

## Account state

State where the account is, in three sentences at most, from what the account said. Name the latest call.

## People and roles

List each named person with the role that someone said they play in the decision, and whether they have joined a call. A job title alone is not a role in the decision. Cite the quote.

## What they want

State what the account said it wants, in its own words. A rep claim that the account did not accept does not count.

## Open items

List each open item, who owns it, and any date that someone stated. Write "no date stated" when there is none.

## Gaps to close in 30 days

List each gap that the calls leave open, such as budget, a missing decision maker, or a technical dependency. Tie each to a quote, and name one action for each. Do not add a gap that no quote supports.

## What does not count

- A role guessed from a title.
- An expansion idea that the account did not raise.
- A rep claim that the account never accepted, such as a promised result.
- A date that nobody stated.
- A gap listed with no supporting quote.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize.

- An account that stated its need, its decision maker, its budget, and a date must produce a short plan with few gaps. Do not pad it with risks.
- A thin input must not get a padded output. When the input supports two items, write two. Do not invent a third to reach a count.

## Sources

The plan follows common account-planning practice. The rules come from reviews of recorded calls.
