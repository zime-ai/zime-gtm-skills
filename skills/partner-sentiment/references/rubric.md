# Partner-Sentiment rubric

This rubric has 6 dimensions. It audits a call with a channel or reseller partner. The partner is the other side of the call, so partner words count the way buyer words count in a sales call.

Three rules apply to every dimension.

- **The partner must say it.** Evidence is something the partner said. A claim that only the vendor's rep makes, such as "they love us", does not count. Partner size and reputation are not evidence of fit. A claim that only the rep makes does not count.
- **Absence is a result.** A dimension that the call never touched is Missed. Do not credit it because the call felt friendly.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each dimension lists the usual false positives.


## 1. Stance stated in the partner's words

**What it is.** The partner says how they see the relationship, in their own words.

**Covered:** the partner states a stance and gives a reason. **Partial:** a general "good" with no reason. **Missed:** no stance is stated.

**Does not count:** "it is going fine" with no reason. The rep's own summary of the relationship.

**A strong call** asks "what would you tell a colleague about working with us?".

## 2. Action behind the words

**What it is.** The partner did something for the vendor, such as recommending or placing it in an account.

**Covered:** the partner names a deal or account where they pushed the vendor. **Partial:** the partner says they would recommend and names no deal. **Missed:** no action is named.

**Does not count:** praise with no deal named. A promise to recommend later.

**A strong call** asks for the last account where they put the vendor forward.

## 3. Rivals they favor

**What it is.** The partner names the other vendors they carry and when they choose them.

**Covered:** the partner names a rival and a case where they chose it. **Partial:** the partner says they carry others and names none. **Missed:** no rival comes up.

**Does not count:** "other vendors" with nothing behind it. A rival that only the rep names.

**A strong call** asks which vendor they choose when the customer is price-sensitive, and why.

## 4. Friction named with its cause

**What it is.** The partner names what frustrates them and what it cost.

**Covered:** the partner names the friction, its cause and its cost. **Partial:** the partner names a friction with no cause or cost. **Missed:** no friction is raised or asked about.

**Does not count:** "no complaints" when nobody asked. A friction that only the rep names.

**A strong call** asks what the last friction cost them in a deal.

## 5. Long-term intent

**What it is.** The partner says whether they will invest, such as headcount, training or certification.

**Covered:** the partner names an investment and a date or a trigger. **Partial:** the partner says they want to grow it and names a condition, with no resource committed. **Missed:** the partner does not say.

**Does not count:** "we want to grow" with no condition and no resource named.

**A strong call** asks what they would need to commit a named person.

## 6. Dated next step owned by the partner

**What it is.** The call ends with a step that the partner owns and has a date.

**Covered:** the partner names a step they will take and a date. **Partial:** a step is agreed with no date or only the vendor owns it. **Missed:** no step is agreed.

**Does not count:** "check in next quarter" with no date. A step that only the rep owns.

**A strong call** books a date and a partner-owned task before the call ends.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize an audit.

- A call that covered every dimension with the partner's own words must not come back mostly Missed. When it does, widen what counts as evidence.
- A call that never raised a dimension must not get credit for it. When a status rests on a line from a "does not count" list, remove that status.

## Sources

The dimensions follow common channel-management practice. The evidence rules and the strong-call lines come from reviews of recorded partner and sales calls.
