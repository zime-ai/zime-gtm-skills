# Call-Prep rules

Writes the prep from the calls that already happened. It uses nothing about the company from outside the transcripts, and it marks what is still unknown. It is the forward-looking partner of the audit skills, which grade a call that is over.

Three rules apply to every section.

- **Cite or leave it out.** Every claim traces to a quote, a timestamp, or a column and cell. A claim with no cited source does not ship.
- **The buyer or customer must say it.** A claim that only the rep makes does not count unless the other side accepted it. Silence is not a yes.
- **Unknown stays unknown.** When the input does not say it, write "not stated". Do not fill the gap with a guess or with outside knowledge.

## Where we left off

State in two or three sentences what the buyer said about the problem, the date, and the people. Quote each point. Use only buyer words and steps that the buyer accepted.

## Open commitments

List every step that either side promised, with an owner and a date. A step with no owner or no date goes in with the words "no owner" or "no date". A rep promise that the buyer never answered is listed as a rep promise.

## Still unknown

List what the calls never established: the approver, a decision date, success measures, the competition, the procurement steps. Write each as a question to ask. Do not list something the calls already answered.

## Risks to raise

List risks that the buyer's own words support, such as a conflict between the buyer's date and the buyer's review steps, an approver who has never joined a call, or an integration worry. Cite each.

## Proposed agenda and ask

Propose a short agenda of three items at most. State the one ask for the call as a specific action with a date and an owner on both sides. Mark the ask as the rep's proposal, because the buyer has not agreed to it.

## What does not count

- A fact about the company that the transcripts do not state.
- A step that the rep promised and the buyer never accepted, written as agreed.
- A question about something that the calls already answered.
- A risk that only the rep names.
- More than three agenda items.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize.

- A deal with a clear status, owned commitments, and a short list of gaps must produce a short, clean prep. Do not pad it with risks.
- A thin input must not get a padded output. When the input supports two items, write two. Do not invent a third to reach a count.

## Sources

The structure follows common pre-call planning practice. The rules come from reviews of recorded sales calls.
