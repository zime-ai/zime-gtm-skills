# BANT rubric

This rubric has four criteria. It is built for speed. It is a first-pass filter and not a deep qualification. A call can score well here and still fail a later `meddicc` pass once more stakeholders join.

Three rules apply to every criterion.

- **The buyer must say it.** Evidence is something the buyer said. A claim from the rep does not count. A job title is not authority.
- **Absence is a result.** A criterion that the call never touched is Missed. Do not credit it because the call went well in other ways.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each criterion lists the usual false positives. Read that line before you write Covered or Partial.

## Budget

**What it is.** The buyer showed financial readiness for this kind of purchase. The budget is approved, allocated, or realistically available. An exact number is not required. The signal must be more than "we have not thought about it".

**Covered looks like:** a specific figure or range, an approval status, or a clear funding source. Example: "finance signed off on this last quarter". The buyer states it with no hesitation on price. **Partial looks like:** the budget exists but depends on more approval, or the buyer is vague about the amount while saying money is available. **Missed looks like:** no discussion of budget, or the buyer says budget is unavailable, or the buyer raises an explicit affordability objection.

**Does not count:** worry about price on its own. A question about pricing. The rep's own estimate of what the deal costs. Price worry never lifts a score above Missed.

**A strong call** asks how the buyer paid for similar tools before and who approves the spend.

"We would need to find budget" is Partial. "We have roughly $40-60k set aside for this this year" is Covered.

## Authority

**What it is.** The person on the call has a real path to a decision. This person approves the spend or actively advocates and can move the deal to the approver. Being on the call is not enough, and title is not enough.

**Covered looks like:** the person names or confirms the approver by name or title and describes their own role in the approval chain. Or the person is the approver. **Partial looks like:** the person points to someone else who decides ("my VP owns this") without a name, or describes their own influence vaguely. **Missed looks like:** nobody on the call can say who approves, or the person's only stated role has no link to the purchase decision.

**Does not count:** a job title alone. The buyer must say who has the final word. Enthusiasm does not make a person an approver. A person whom others wait on for sign-off is the approver. A person who only recommends is not.

**A strong call** asks what the approver needs to see and whether that person will join the next call.

"I can move this forward, but final sign-off is my VP's" is Partial. Naming that VP and describing what they need to see is Covered.

## Need

**What it is.** A specific problem that the buyer described in their own words and called real and relevant. The rep did not raise it or assume it. It is not general interest in the category.

**Covered looks like:** a concrete pain tied to a root cause or a business impact. The buyer described it without prompting or returned to it more than once. **Partial looks like:** a real problem named once, with no detail on cause or impact. **Missed looks like:** only "we are always looking to improve" category-level interest, with no specific problem that the buyer owns.

**Does not count:** a problem that the rep names and the buyer never raised. A complaint about a vendor the buyer uses today. A problem that vanishes once no vendor exists.

**A strong call** asks for the last real example of the problem and what it cost.

## Timeline

**What it is.** A real reason exists for the buyer to decide by a specific point. The reason can be a renewal date, a budget cycle, a triggering event, or an active competing evaluation. "Sometime this year" is the weak case.

**Covered looks like:** a named date, deadline, or renewal window tied to a reason that it matters. **Partial looks like:** a rough window ("this quarter, probably") with no reason behind it. **Missed looks like:** no timeline surfaced, or "no real urgency" stated outright. Vague timelines are one of the most common reasons that a qualified-looking deal goes quiet.

**Does not count:** a date for the next meeting. A hypothetical, such as "how long does an evaluation take?". "Maybe next quarter", which is Partial at best.

**A strong call** asks what fixes the date and what a slip costs.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize an audit.

- A first call that surfaced strong signal on all four criteria must not come back mostly Partial. When it does, loosen what counts as Covered.
- A call that never raised a criterion must not get credit for it. When a status rests on a line from a "does not count" list, remove that status.

## Sources

The four criteria follow the BANT framework that sales teams widely teach. The evidence rules and the "strong call" lines come from reviews of recorded sales calls.
