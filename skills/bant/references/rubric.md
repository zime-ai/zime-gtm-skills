# BANT rubric

Four criteria, built for speed. This is a first-pass filter, not a deep
qualification — a call can score well here and still fail a later `meddicc`
pass once more stakeholders are involved.

## Budget

**What it is.** Whether the prospect confirmed financial readiness for this
kind of purchase — an approved, allocated, or realistically available
budget or funding source — not necessarily an exact number, but more than
"we haven't thought about it."

**Covered looks like:** a specific figure or range, an approval status
("finance signed off on this last quarter"), or a clear funding source,
stated with no hesitation on price. **Partial looks like:** budget exists
but is conditional on further approval or stakeholder alignment, or the
prospect is vague about the amount while confirming money is available.
**Missed looks like:** no discussion of budget at all, or the prospect
states budget is unavailable/exhausted, or raises an explicit affordability
objection.

"We'd need to find budget" is Partial; "we have roughly $40-60k set aside
for this this year" is Covered.

## Authority

**What it is.** Whether the person on the call has a real path to a
decision — either as the one who can approve spend, or as an active
advocate who can move it to the person who can. Not simply "is on the
call," and not simply title.

**Covered looks like:** the person on the call names or confirms the
approver by name/title and describes their own role in the approval chain,
or is themself confirmed as the approver. **Partial looks like:** the
person references someone else who decides ("my VP owns this") without
naming them, or describes their own influence vaguely. **Missed looks
like:** no one on the call can describe who approves this, or the person's
only stated role is unrelated to the purchase decision.

A prospect saying "I can move this forward but final sign-off is my VP's"
is Partial; naming that VP and describing what they'd need to see is
Covered.

## Need

**What it is.** A specific problem the prospect described in their own
words and acknowledged as real and relevant — not a challenge the rep
raised or assumed, and not generic interest in the category.

**Covered looks like:** a concrete pain tied to a root cause or business
impact, described unprompted or returned to more than once. **Partial
looks like:** a real problem named once, without detail on cause or
impact. **Missed looks like:** only "we're always looking to improve"
category-level interest, with no specific problem the prospect owns.

## Timeline

**What it is.** Whether there's a real reason this needs to be decided by
a specific point — a renewal date, a budget cycle, a triggering event, or
an active competing evaluation — versus "sometime this year" being the
most specific thing said.

**Covered looks like:** a named date, deadline, or renewal window tied to a
reason it matters. **Partial looks like:** a rough window ("this quarter,
probably") with no anchor to why. **Missed looks like:** no timeline
surfaced, or "no real urgency" stated outright. Vague timelines are one of
the most common reasons an apparently-qualified deal goes quiet.

## Reads-well-too check

A first call that clearly surfaced strong signal on all four criteria should
not come back mostly Partial just because BANT is being applied strictly. If
that happens on a call that plainly went well, loosen what counts as
Covered before finalizing.
