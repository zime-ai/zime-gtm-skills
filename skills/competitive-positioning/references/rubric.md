# Competitive-Positioning rubric

This rubric has six dimensions. They show whether the rep positioned against a named rival with the buyer's own criteria, proof, honesty about gaps, and a test.

Three rules apply to every dimension.

- **Quote the right speaker.** Evidence is something the right speaker said. For what the rep did, quote the rep. For what the buyer cares about or reacted to, the buyer must say it. A claim that only the rep makes does not count.
- **Absence is a result.** A dimension that the call never touched is Missed. Do not credit it because the call felt friendly.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each dimension lists the usual false positives.


## 1. Rival named by the buyer

**What it is.** The buyer names the alternative they compare and how far along it is.

**Covered:** the buyer names the rival or states that they will do nothing, and says how far the evaluation has gone. **Partial:** the buyer mentions "other options" and names none. **Missed:** no alternative comes up on a contestable deal.

**Does not count:** a rival that only the rep names. An evaluation that ended long ago, unless the buyer says what it taught them.

**A strong call** asks which options the buyer looks at, including doing nothing.

## 2. Buyer's criteria heard

**What it is.** The buyer states what matters in the comparison.

**Covered:** the buyer names criteria and says which are must-haves. **Partial:** one loose preference with no rank. **Missed:** no criteria surface.

**Does not count:** criteria that the rep lists. A reaction to a demo.

**A strong call** asks the buyer to rank the criteria.

## 3. Difference tied to a criterion

**What it is.** The rep states one specific difference and ties it to a criterion that the buyer named.

**Covered:** the rep states a specific difference and links it to a stated criterion. **Partial:** the rep says "better" or "easier" with no link to a criterion. **Missed:** the rep lists features, or says nothing about the rival.

**Does not count:** praise for the product. Criticism of the rival. A claim with no link to what the buyer said matters.

**A strong call** says "you told me X matters, and here is how we differ on X".

## 4. Proof offered

**What it is.** The rep backs the difference with proof of a kind that the buyer trusts, and the buyer reacts.

**Covered:** the rep offers a reference, a number, or a live test, and the buyer responds to it. **Partial:** the rep makes a claim with no proof. **Missed:** no proof is offered.

**Does not count:** a benchmark that the vendor set. A customer logo with no result. "Our customers love us".

**A strong call** offers a reference from a buyer of the same kind.

## 5. Gap handled honestly

**What it is.** When the rival does something the product does not, the rep says so plainly.

**Covered:** the rep states the gap and gives an owner and a date or a workaround, and the buyer accepts it. **Partial:** the rep acknowledges the gap and gives no date or workaround. **Missed:** the buyer raises a gap and the rep dodges or changes the subject.

**Does not count:** "we are working on it" with no date. A promise to find out with no owner and no due date.

**A strong call** turns the gap into a yes-or-no test the buyer can run.

## 6. Buyer tests the difference

**What it is.** The buyer agrees to a step that tests the claimed difference.

**Covered:** the buyer agrees to a trial, a reference call, or a test with a date. **Partial:** the buyer shows interest and agrees to no step. **Missed:** no step follows.

**Does not count:** polite nods. "Sounds good" with no step.

**A strong call** proposes the test and a date before the call ends.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize an audit.

- A call where the rep tied a difference to the buyer's criteria, offered proof, named the gap, and booked a test must not come back mostly Missed. When it does, widen what counts as evidence.
- A call that never raised a dimension must not get credit for it. When a status rests on a line from a "does not count" list, remove that status.

## Sources

The dimensions follow common competitive-selling practice. The evidence rules and the strong-call lines come from reviews of recorded sales calls.
