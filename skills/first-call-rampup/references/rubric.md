# First-call rampup rubric

Six foundational techniques for a rep still early in ramp. This is a
coaching lens — the point is building the skill, not judging the deal.

## 1. Strong opening

**What it is.** Whether the rep frames the call up front — an agenda, a
reason to keep the prospect's attention — and gets some real signal that
the prospect is actually engaged, rather than launching straight into
questions or a pitch with no framing at all.

**Covered looks like:** the rep states what the call will cover and the
prospect gives some clear signal back — agrees to the plan, asks a
clarifying question, confirms the time is good. **Partial looks like:** the
rep opens with some framing but the prospect's response is minimal — a
one-word "okay" with no real engagement. **Missed looks like:** no framing
at all, straight into questions or product, with no read on whether the
prospect is actually ready to engage.

"So, tell me about your company" cold, with no framing, is Missed; "before
we dive in, I wanted to walk through what we usually cover and then hear
what's top of mind for you — sound good?" followed by the prospect
confirming is Covered.

## 2. Open-ended questioning

**What it is.** Whether the rep's questions genuinely invite the prospect
to describe something in their own words, versus questions that can be
answered yes/no and tend to stop the conversation rather than open it up.

**Covered looks like:** questions built around "tell me about...", "walk me
through...", "what happens when..." that draw out detail. **Partial looks
like:** a mix, with closed questions outnumbering open ones. **Missed looks
like:** almost entirely closed or leading questions ("so you'd want
something faster, right?") that hand the prospect a yes/no and move on.

"So you're using spreadsheets for this right now?" is closed and, alone,
Missed; "walk me through what happens today when that request comes in" is
Covered — it hands the floor to the prospect.

## 3. Pain & priority uncovered

**What it is.** Whether the rep pushed past the first vague answer to
something the prospect actually named as a real, owned problem, rather
than accepting "we're always looking to improve" and moving on.

**Covered looks like:** a specific problem, described in the prospect's
own words, that the rep followed up on rather than let pass. **Partial
looks like:** a real problem is named but the rep doesn't probe it further.
**Missed looks like:** only generic category-level interest, with the rep
not pushing for anything more specific.

"We're always looking to get more efficient" left unprobed is Missed;
following it with "what does that look like today — where's the time
actually going?" and getting a specific answer is Covered.

## 4. Trigger surfaced

**What it is.** Whether the rep asked, in some form, why the prospect is
looking at this now rather than six months ago or six months from now —
a real triggering event or change, not just general interest.

**Covered looks like:** the rep asks directly or indirectly and gets a
specific event, deadline, or change named as the reason for the timing.
**Partial looks like:** the rep asks but the prospect's answer stays vague
("just felt like the right time"). **Missed looks like:** the question
never comes up at all.

"So why look at this now instead of last year?" met with "our lease
renewal forced us to rethink the whole floor plan" is Covered; the same
question never being asked is Missed.

## 5. Pitch clarity

**What it is.** When the rep did describe the product, whether it was
specific and tied back to something the prospect actually said earlier in
the call, versus a generic recitation of features that could apply to any
prospect.

**Covered looks like:** the rep names a capability and explicitly connects
it to a problem or word the prospect used. **Partial looks like:** the
pitch is accurate but generic, with no callback to anything specific the
prospect said. **Missed looks like:** a feature list with no connection to
the conversation at all.

"We also have reporting, and integrations, and an API" recited on its own
is Missed-to-Partial; "you mentioned the scheduling conflicts happen
mostly on the crew side — that's exactly what our conflict-detection view
is built for" is Covered.

## 6. Rapport

**What it is.** Whether the call reads as a genuine two-way conversation —
the prospect volunteering something beyond what was asked — or the rep
working through a script while the prospect gives short, closed answers.
Standard sales-coaching guidance treats this as one of the earliest
readable signals of a call's real health, independent of what got said.

**Covered looks like:** the prospect offers unprompted context, a reaction,
or a story of their own. **Partial looks like:** the prospect answers only
when asked, with minimal detail. **Missed looks like:** short, closed
answers throughout, with the rep doing most of the talking.

A prospect who answers "yep" and "sure" throughout is Missed; one who adds
"actually, that reminds me — we had a similar issue last quarter with a
different vendor" unprompted is Covered.

## Reads-well-too check

Before finalizing, consider whether this read would feel fair and
motivating to the rep, not just accurate. A new rep who did several things
well and one thing not-yet-there should hear that ratio reflected in the
output, not a wall of Missed items.
