# Discovery-call rubric

Nine dimensions of what a thorough B2B discovery call covers. Each entry
below is a fresh description of the dimension, not a script — a call can
cover it in any order, in any words, and still score as Covered.

## 1. Pain & business impact

Did the rep get the prospect to describe a specific problem in their own
words, and did they push past the first vague answer to quantify it — cost,
time, risk, or a number the prospect volunteered? "We have some inefficiency
in that process" is a Partial at best; "that costs us about 10 hours a week
across the team" is Covered.

## 2. Current state & tooling

What does the prospect do today to handle this problem — a competitor tool,
a manual process, a spreadsheet, nothing at all? A call that never
establishes the baseline can't credibly claim the new solution improves on
it.

## 3. Trigger — why now

Why is this prospect looking at a solution *this quarter* rather than last
year or next year? A missing trigger is one of the most common reasons a
deal that looked healthy at discovery stalls later — there was no real
urgency, just curiosity.

## 4. Desired outcome & success criteria

Did the prospect articulate what "solved" looks like, ideally in terms they
could measure later — not "make things better" but a specific outcome they'd
recognize as a win.

## 5. Decision process & stakeholders

Who else is involved in deciding, what does the internal process look like,
and did the rep map this beyond the person on the call? A discovery call
that ends without knowing whether the person on the call can actually say
yes is incomplete.

## 6. Budget & timeline

Not "what's your budget" as a single blunt question, but whether the call
surfaced any real signal on what this problem is worth solving and when a
decision needs to land.

## 7. Procurement & buying path

For anything beyond a very small deal: does the rep have any sense of the
prospect's procurement process — security review, legal, vendor
onboarding — before it becomes a late-stage surprise?

## 8. Competitive landscape

Is the prospect evaluating anything else, formally or informally, and did
the rep find out without being asked directly ("who else are you talking
to") landing as adversarial?

## 9. Rapport & engagement

Was this a genuine two-way conversation, or a rep working through a script
while the prospect gave short answers? Low engagement here is often the
earliest signal that the deal isn't as live as it looks on paper.

## Reads-well-too check

Before finalizing an audit, sanity-check it against a call you already know
covered discovery well. If the rubric still returns several Missed
dimensions on a genuinely good call, the scoring is biased toward finding
fault — loosen what counts as evidence for Covered/Partial before shipping
the read.
