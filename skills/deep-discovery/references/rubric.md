# Discovery-call rubric

Nine dimensions of what a thorough B2B discovery call covers. Each entry
below is a fresh description of the dimension, not a script — a call can
cover it in any order, in any words, and still score as Covered.

## 1. Pain & business impact

**What it is.** Whether the rep got the prospect to describe a specific
problem in their own words, acknowledged as real, and pushed past the
first vague answer to a business impact — cost, time, risk, or a number
the prospect volunteered.

**Covered:** a concrete pain tied to a quantified impact, described
unprompted or returned to more than once. **Partial:** a real problem
named once, without a cause or a number attached. **Missed:** only generic
"we're always looking to improve" language, with no specific owned problem.

"We have some inefficiency in that process" is a Partial at best; "that
costs us about 10 hours a week across the team" is Covered.

## 2. Current state & tooling

**What it is.** What the prospect does today to handle this problem — a
competitor tool, a manual process, a spreadsheet, nothing at all.

**Covered:** the current tool/process is named specifically, with enough
detail to compare against. **Partial:** a current approach is named
generically ("we use spreadsheets") without workflow detail. **Missed:**
the call never establishes any baseline — a solution can't credibly claim
to improve on the unknown.

## 3. Trigger — why now

**What it is.** Why this prospect is looking at a solution this quarter
rather than last year or next year — an event, mandate, or change that
increased urgency, not general curiosity.

**Covered:** a specific event, deadline, or change is named and tied to
the decision to act now. **Partial:** urgency is asserted ("we need this
soon") without a specific triggering event. **Missed:** no reason surfaces
for why now versus any other time — often the earliest sign a deal that
looks healthy will stall later.

## 4. Desired outcome & success criteria

**What it is.** What the prospect said "solved" looks like, ideally in
terms they could measure later — not "make things better" but a specific,
recognizable win.

**Covered:** a measurable target or a description specific enough to check
later. **Partial:** an outcome is described but stays qualitative ("just
need it to be easier"). **Missed:** no outcome is ever articulated beyond
interest in the category.

## 5. Decision process & stakeholders

**What it is.** Who else is involved in deciding — the champion, the
economic buyer, other approvers — and whether the rep mapped this beyond
the person on the call.

**Covered:** the economic buyer or other approvers are named, with some
sense of the approval sequence. **Partial:** another stakeholder is
referenced vaguely ("my team would need to weigh in") without being named.
**Missed:** the call ends without knowing whether the person on the call
can actually say yes.

## 6. Budget & timeline

**What it is.** Not "what's your budget" as a single blunt question, but
whether the call surfaced real signal on what this problem is worth
solving and when a decision needs to land.

**Covered:** a figure, range, or approval status, plus a specific decision
date or window. **Partial:** one of the two surfaces (budget signal without
a date, or vice versa). **Missed:** neither surfaces — "sometime this
year, not sure on cost" is the most specific thing said.

## 7. Procurement & buying path

**What it is.** For anything beyond a very small deal: whether the rep has
any sense of the prospect's procurement process — security review, legal,
vendor onboarding — before it becomes a late-stage surprise.

**Covered:** a specific procurement step is named (security review,
specific approval body). **Partial:** procurement is acknowledged as
existing but not detailed. **Missed:** never comes up, on a deal size
where it plausibly matters.

## 8. Competitive landscape

**What it is.** Whether the prospect is evaluating anything else,
formally or informally, and whether the rep found this out without a
blunt "who else are you talking to" landing as adversarial.

**Covered:** a specific alternative (named competitor or "doing nothing")
surfaces, with some sense of how it's viewed. **Partial:** the prospect
mentions evaluating options without naming any. **Missed:** competition
never comes up despite the deal being contestable.

## 9. Rapport & engagement

**What it is.** Whether this was a genuine two-way conversation — the
prospect opening up beyond transactional exchanges, reciprocating with
context or research of their own — or a rep working through a script
while the prospect gave short answers.

**Covered:** the prospect volunteers something beyond what was asked —
context, a story, a reaction — unprompted. **Partial:** the prospect
answers only when prompted, with minimal, generic detail. **Missed:**
straight to business, short closed answers throughout, rep dominates the
talk time. Low engagement here is often the earliest signal a deal isn't
as live as it looks on paper.

## Reads-well-too check

Before finalizing an audit, sanity-check it against a call you already know
covered discovery well. If the rubric still returns several Missed
dimensions on a genuinely good call, the scoring is biased toward finding
fault — loosen what counts as evidence for Covered/Partial before shipping
the read.
