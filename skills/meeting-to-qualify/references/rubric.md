# Qualification-call rubric

This rubric has eight dimensions. They separate a real, worth-pursuing deal from one that looks active and is not. It is a go or no-go lens, not a deep-discovery pass. It goes lighter on pain and sharper on whether the deal holds up.

Three rules apply to every dimension.

- **The buyer must say it.** Evidence is something the buyer said. A claim from the rep does not count. A job title is not authority.
- **Absence is a result.** A dimension that the call never touched is Missed. Do not credit it because the meeting felt friendly.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each dimension lists the usual false positives. Read that line before you write Covered or Partial.

## 1. Problem confirmed as real and urgent

**What it is.** The buyer confirmed an active priority in their own words and gave a reason it needs solving now. A polite "sounds interesting, send me something" about a problem the rep raised is the weak case.

**Covered looks like:** a specific problem tied to a business consequence of leaving it unsolved. **Partial looks like:** a real problem named once with no consequence. **Missed looks like:** only polite interest, with no problem that the buyer owns.

**Does not count:** a problem that the rep names and the buyer never raised. "That is something to look at" with no owner. Interest without urgency is the most common false positive in a qualified pipeline.

**A strong call** asks what the cost is of no action this quarter.

"We are opening four new stores in Q1 and our current process does not scale" is Covered. "Yeah, that is something we could look at" is Missed.

## 2. Buying authority mapped

**What it is.** The person on the call can say yes, or the buyer clearly named who can. It includes any approval threshold that applies.

**Covered looks like:** the person names the approver and describes their own role in the chain. Or the person is the approver. **Partial looks like:** an approver is named but has not agreed, or the threshold is vague. **Missed looks like:** nobody on the call can say who approves.

**Does not count:** a job title alone. The buyer must say who has the final word. Enthusiasm does not make a person an approver.

**A strong call** asks whether the approver will join the next call and what that person needs to see.

"I own this decision operationally, but anything over $75k goes through our VP for sign-off" is Partial. The same statement plus the VP joining a working session is Covered.

## 3. Budget plausibility

**What it is.** This is a plausibility test and not a confirmed number. The question is whether the buyer can fund a purchase at a realistic price point.

**Covered looks like:** a stated figure, range, or approval threshold that implies real spending authority. **Partial looks like:** the buyer implies spending capacity and never states it. **Missed looks like:** budget is unknown, or the buyer says funding is not available.

**Does not count:** worry about price on its own. A question about pricing. The size of the buyer's company.

**A strong call** asks how the buyer paid for similar tools and who approved them.

## 4. Timeline reality

**What it is.** A specific event, deadline, or process drives the decision by a particular point. "Sometime this year" is the weak case.

**Covered looks like:** a named date tied to a reason that it matters. **Partial looks like:** a rough window with no anchor. **Missed looks like:** no timeline surfaces.

**Does not count:** a date for the next meeting. A hypothetical about how long a typical evaluation takes.

**A strong call** asks what fixes the date and what a slip costs.

"We need to sign by end of next month to roll it out before the Q1 store openings" is Covered. "Sometime this year, probably" is Partial at best.

## 5. Fit with what is being sold

**What it is.** The buyer's current tools and stated need line up with the offer. The weak case is a mismatch that the rep skipped over to keep the deal moving.

**Covered looks like:** the buyer's stated need maps directly onto the core capability on sale. **Partial looks like:** a partial or adjacent fit with a gap that the rep has not addressed. **Missed looks like:** a fit gap large enough that the deal likely fails its own evaluation later.

**Does not count:** the rep saying "this fits" while the buyer says nothing. A capability that the buyer asked about and the rep promised to look into.

**A strong call** states fit gaps early and plainly, with an owner and a date for each.

## 6. Competitive situation

**What it is.** Whether the buyer seriously evaluates alternatives or only takes a meeting.

**Covered looks like:** a specific alternative is named, with some read on how it compares. **Partial looks like:** the buyer mentions an evaluation and names nothing. **Missed looks like:** competition never comes up on a deal size where it plausibly matters. A buyer who knows no alternatives this early sometimes is not seriously shopping yet. Read it in context.

**Does not count:** an evaluation that ended long ago. "Other options" with nothing behind it is Partial.

**A strong call** asks which option the buyer prefers today and what gap that option has.

## 7. Engagement as a signal

**What it is.** The call was a genuine two-way conversation. The buyer volunteers context, reacts, and asks questions back. The weak case is minimal answers while the rep carries the call.

**Covered looks like:** the buyer volunteers detail beyond what was asked. **Partial looks like:** the buyer answers only after a prompt, with little elaboration. **Missed looks like:** short, closed answers throughout, with no engagement in return. Low engagement at the qualification stage is a stronger warning than at discovery, because the buyer had more chance to invest.

**Does not count:** politeness. Small talk that only the rep makes.

## 8. Concrete next step secured

**What it is.** The call ended with a specific next step that both sides agreed to. It can be a date, a person to bring in, or a document to review. "Let us stay in touch" is the weak case.

**Covered looks like:** a named date, a named attendee, and a clear purpose for the next touch. **Partial looks like:** a next step agreed in principle with no date and no owner. **Missed looks like:** no follow-up secured. No concrete next step is often the clearest sign that the deal is not real yet.

**Does not count:** "let us reconnect sometime". A promise by the rep to send a summary with no time given.

**A strong call** ends with a proposed day, the people who must attend, and an agenda.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize an audit.

- A call that you know was a strong, real deal must not come back mostly Missed. When it does, the read is too strict, so widen what counts as evidence.
- A call that never raised a dimension must not get credit for it. When a status rests on a line from a "does not count" list, remove that status.

## Sources

The dimensions follow common qualification practice. The evidence rules and the "strong call" lines come from reviews of recorded sales calls.
