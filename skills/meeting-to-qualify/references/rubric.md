# Qualification-call rubric

Eight dimensions that separate a real, worth-pursuing deal from one that
looks active but isn't. This is a go/no-go lens, not a deep-discovery pass —
shallower on pain exploration, sharper on whether the deal itself holds up.

## 1. Problem confirmed as real and urgent

**What it is.** Whether the prospect confirmed this is an active priority,
in their own words, with a reason it needs solving now — not a "sounds
interesting, send me something" response to a problem the rep raised.

**Covered looks like:** a specific problem tied to a business consequence
if it isn't solved. **Partial looks like:** a real problem is named once
without a consequence attached. **Missed looks like:** only polite
interest, with no owned problem.

"We're opening four new stores in Q1 and our current process just doesn't
scale" is Covered; "yeah, that's something we could look at" is Missed.
Vague interest without urgency is the single most common false-positive in
a qualified pipeline.

## 2. Buying authority mapped

**What it is.** Whether the person on the call can say yes, or clearly
named who can, including any approval threshold that applies.

**Covered looks like:** the person names the approver and describes their
own role in the chain, or is themself the approver. **Partial looks like:**
an approver is named but hasn't confirmed alignment, or the threshold is
vague. **Missed looks like:** no one on the call can describe who approves
this.

"I own this decision operationally, but anything over $75k goes through
our VP for sign-off" is Partial until the VP is looped in and confirms
alignment; the same statement plus the VP joining a working session moves
it to Covered.

## 3. Budget plausibility

**What it is.** Not a confirmed number — a plausibility check on whether
this prospect could actually fund a purchase at a realistic price point.

**Covered looks like:** a stated figure, range, or approval threshold that
implies real spending authority. **Partial looks like:** spending capacity
is implied but never stated directly. **Missed looks like:** budget is
entirely unknown, or the prospect states funding isn't available.

## 4. Timeline reality

**What it is.** Whether there's a specific event, deadline, or process
driving a decision by a particular point, versus an open-ended "sometime
this year."

**Covered looks like:** a named date tied to a reason it matters. **Partial
looks like:** a rough window with no anchor. **Missed looks like:** no
timeline surfaces at all.

"We'd need to sign by end of next month to have time to roll it out before
the Q1 store openings" is Covered; "sometime this year, probably" is
Partial at best.

## 5. Fit with what's being sold

**What it is.** Whether the prospect's current tooling and stated need
actually line up with what's being offered, or there's a mismatch the rep
glossed over to keep the deal moving.

**Covered looks like:** the prospect's stated need maps directly onto the
core capability being sold. **Partial looks like:** a partial or adjacent
fit, with a gap the rep hasn't addressed. **Missed looks like:** a fit gap
significant enough that the deal would likely fail its own evaluation
later.

## 6. Competitive situation

**What it is.** Whether the prospect is seriously evaluating this against
alternatives, or is just taking a meeting.

**Covered looks like:** a specific alternative is named, with some read on
how it compares. **Partial looks like:** evaluation is mentioned without
naming anything. **Missed looks like:** competition never comes up on a
deal size where it plausibly matters. A prospect with zero awareness of
alternatives this early is sometimes a sign they aren't seriously shopping
yet, not automatically a red flag — read it in context.

## 7. Engagement as a signal

**What it is.** Whether this was a genuine two-way conversation — the
prospect volunteering context, reacting, asking questions back — versus
giving minimal answers while the rep carried the call.

**Covered looks like:** the prospect volunteers detail beyond what was
asked. **Partial looks like:** the prospect answers only when prompted,
with minimal elaboration. **Missed looks like:** short, closed answers
throughout with no reciprocal engagement. Low engagement at the
qualification stage is a stronger warning sign than at discovery, because
there's been more chance to invest.

## 8. Concrete next step secured

**What it is.** Whether the call ended with a specific next step both
sides agreed to — a date, a person to loop in, a document to review —
versus a vague "let's stay in touch."

**Covered looks like:** a named date, named attendee, and a clear purpose
for the next touch. **Partial looks like:** a next step is agreed in
principle without a date or owner. **Missed looks like:** no follow-up is
secured. No concrete next step is often the clearest single tell that the
deal isn't real yet.

## Reads-well-too check

Before finalizing, sanity-check against a call you already know was a
strong, real deal. If it still comes back mostly Missed, the read is tuned
too strictly — loosen what counts as evidence before reporting.
