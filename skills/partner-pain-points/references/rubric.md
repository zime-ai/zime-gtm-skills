# Partner-Pain-Points rubric

This rubric has 7 dimensions. It audits a call with a channel or reseller partner. The partner is the other side of the call, so partner words count the way buyer words count in a sales call.

Three rules apply to every dimension.

- **The partner must say it.** Evidence is something the partner said. A claim that only the vendor's rep makes, such as "they love us", does not count. Partner size and reputation are not evidence of fit. A claim that only the rep makes does not count.
- **Absence is a result.** A dimension that the call never touched is Missed. Do not credit it because the call felt friendly.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each dimension lists the usual false positives.


## 1. The partner's own problem named

**What it is.** The partner names a problem in their own business.

**Covered:** the partner names a specific problem in their own words. **Partial:** a general area such as "selling is hard". **Missed:** no problem comes up.

**Does not count:** a problem the rep names. A complaint about the vendor's product.

**A strong call** asks what is hardest in running the business, not in selling the vendor.

## 2. Cost to the partner

**What it is.** The partner states what the problem costs in money, time or deals.

**Covered:** the partner gives a figure or a count. **Partial:** the partner says it costs them and gives no figure. **Missed:** no cost comes up.

**Does not count:** "it hurts us" with no figure. A cost that only the rep estimates.

**A strong call** asks how many deals or hours the problem cost last quarter.

## 3. Who feels it

**What it is.** The partner names the roles that carry the problem.

**Covered:** the partner names a role and what it stops them doing. **Partial:** the partner says "my team". **Missed:** nobody is named.

**Does not count:** a job title with no stated effect.

**A strong call** asks who stops doing their real work because of it.

## 4. Root cause

**What it is.** The partner says why the problem exists.

**Covered:** the partner states a cause. **Partial:** the partner guesses a cause. **Missed:** no cause comes up.

**Does not count:** a cause that the rep supplies.

**A strong call** asks why the problem happens and what makes it worse.

## 5. Prior attempt to fix it

**What it is.** The partner says what they already tried.

**Covered:** the partner names an attempt and why it failed. **Partial:** the partner says they tried something and gives no detail. **Missed:** the call never asks.

**Does not count:** "we looked at it" with nothing behind it.

**A strong call** asks what they built or bought before, and what broke.

## 6. What would help, in the partner's words

**What it is.** The partner states the specific help that they want.

**Covered:** the partner names a specific tool, change or support. **Partial:** the partner names a wish with no shape. **Missed:** no help is named.

**Does not count:** a feature that the rep proposes and the partner only accepts.

**A strong call** asks what a good fix looks like and how they would know it worked.

## 7. Dated next step owned by both sides

**What it is.** The call ends with a step that both sides own and a date.

**Covered:** both sides own a step and a date. **Partial:** a step is agreed with no date or only one owner. **Missed:** no step is agreed.

**Does not count:** "I will see what exists" with no date. "Okay" from the partner.

**A strong call** books a date and a partner-owned task before the call ends.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize an audit.

- A call that covered every dimension with the partner's own words must not come back mostly Missed. When it does, widen what counts as evidence.
- A call that never raised a dimension must not get credit for it. When a status rests on a line from a "does not count" list, remove that status.

## Sources

The dimensions follow common channel-management practice. The evidence rules and the strong-call lines come from reviews of recorded partner and sales calls.
