# Healthcare / life sciences vertical pack

**Unreviewed** — see `SKILL.md`'s "Status: unreviewed" note.

## Typical buyer titles / who signs

Practice Manager or Office Manager often runs point for smaller practices;
larger groups route through a VP of Operations or CMIO (Chief Medical
Information Officer) for anything touching clinical workflow or patient
data, with a CFO or administrator approving spend. Clinical staff
(physicians, nurses) are frequently influencers, rarely economic buyers.

## Compliance / regulatory drivers

HIPAA (any tool touching PHI triggers a security/compliance review), state
licensing-board reporting requirements, payer/insurer audit requests, and —
for larger systems — Joint Commission accreditation cycles. Compliance
pressure here often arrives as "we got asked about this in an audit," not a
proactive initiative.

## Specialized pain categories (variant of the generic five buckets)

1. **Compliance and audit pressure** — an audit finding, a payer request, a
   licensing review surfaced a gap
2. **Clinical workflow friction** — manual processes that pull clinical or
   admin staff away from patient-facing work
3. **Data / documentation risk** — incomplete records, delayed
   documentation, anything that could surface in an audit or a claim dispute
4. **Staffing / burnout pressure** — clinical or admin staff stretched thin,
   turnover risk
5. **Leadership / board pressure** — a practice owner, medical director, or
   health-system leadership pushing for a fix

## Domain vocabulary

PHI (protected health information), HIPAA, BAA (business associate
agreement — often required before any vendor touches PHI), EHR/EMR
integration, prior authorization, claims, credentialing, patient
throughput.

## Procurement shape

A signed BAA is frequently a hard gate before any pilot involving real
patient data can start — this can stall a deal that otherwise looks ready,
independent of budget or interest. Surface this early rather than assuming
procurement is purely commercial.

## Disqualifying signal

The prospect cannot name who owns a BAA/compliance sign-off internally —
often a sign the deal is being driven by an enthusiastic individual
contributor with no clear path through the compliance gate.
