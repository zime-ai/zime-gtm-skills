# Rep-Ramp-Status-Digest rules

Reads one rep's calls in time order. It states a trend only when at least two calls show the technique, and it counts a technique as improved only when the buyer's replies show it worked. It uses the six techniques that `first-call-rampup` audits.

Three rules apply to every section.

- **Cite or leave it out.** Every claim traces to a quote, a timestamp, or a column and cell. A claim with no cited source does not ship.
- **The buyer or customer must say it.** A claim that only the rep makes does not count unless the other side accepted it. Silence is not a yes.
- **Unknown stays unknown.** When the input does not say it, write "not stated". Do not fill the gap with a guess or with outside knowledge.

## Scope

State the rep, the number of calls, and the weeks that they cover.

## Status by technique

For each of the six techniques (opening, open-ended questioning, pain and priority, trigger, pitch clarity, rapport) give a status: improving, flat, regressing, or not enough evidence. Quote the rep in each call that shows the technique, and the buyer's reply. A technique shown in fewer than two calls is not enough evidence.

## What changed

Name the one or two changes between the first and the latest call that the quotes show. Do not name a change that only one call supports.

## Next focus

Name the one technique with the most room to improve and the question to practice. Mark it as the writer's suggestion.

## Not yet judged

List the techniques that have not been shown in enough calls to judge.

## What does not count

- A trait of the rep, such as confidence, with no quote.
- An improvement that the buyer's reply does not support.
- A trend from one call.
- More than one next focus.
- A comparison with another rep.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize.

- A rep whose calls are flat must get a status that says flat. Do not invent progress to encourage the rep.
- A thin input must not get a padded output. When the input supports two items, write two. Do not invent a third to reach a count.

## Sources

The techniques follow common new-rep onboarding practice. The rules come from reviews of recorded sales calls.
