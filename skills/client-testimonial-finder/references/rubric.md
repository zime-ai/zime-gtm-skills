# Client-Testimonial-Finder rules

Reads a customer call. A quotable line is specific, positive, and said by the customer. A hedged line, a conditional line, a line the customer said off the record, and any line the rep said are not usable. Every quote needs the customer's permission before use.

Three rules apply to every section.

- **Cite or leave it out.** Every claim traces to a quote, a timestamp, or a column and cell. A claim with no cited source does not ship.
- **The buyer or customer must say it.** A claim that only the rep makes does not count unless the other side accepted it. Silence is not a yes.
- **Unknown stays unknown.** When the input does not say it, write "not stated". Do not fill the gap with a guess or with outside knowledge.

## Quotable lines

List each customer line that is specific, positive, and clear, as an exact quote with the speaker and what it shows. A number or a named outcome makes a line stronger.

## Not usable

List each line that you rejected and the reason: said by the rep, hedged, conditional, off the record, or too vague. Quote it.

## Needs permission

State that every quote needs the customer's approval of the exact wording. Name any line that holds a number, a name, or a role. State what the customer said about permission.

## Best three

Rank up to three quotable lines by how specific they are. List fewer if the call supports fewer.

## Questions to ask the customer

Write the questions that would turn a weak line into a usable one, and the question that asks for permission.

## What does not count

- A line the rep said, even if the customer agreed.
- A line the customer said off the record.
- A line with a condition, such as a rating the customer would give if something changed.
- A vague line such as 'it is fine'.
- A paraphrase written as an exact quote.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize.

- A call where the customer gave several specific, positive lines must produce a list of quotable lines. Do not reject a line only because it is short.
- A thin input must not get a padded output. When the input supports two items, write two. Do not invent a third to reach a count.

## Sources

The rules follow common customer-reference practice. They come from reviews of recorded customer calls.
