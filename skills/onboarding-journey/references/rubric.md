# Onboarding-journey rubric

Five dimensions for a new-customer onboarding check-in. Kept short
deliberately — onboarding calls are shorter and more procedural than a
sales call, and a longer rubric here would be inventing signal that isn't
there.

## 1. Onboarding experience quality

**What it is.** Whether setup is going smoothly from the customer's side,
versus still fighting basic configuration issues past when that should be
resolved.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a specific account of what's
smooth or what's stuck. A miss looks like onboarding progress left
unaddressed or described only as "going fine" with no detail.

**Weak:** "Setup's basically done." **Strong:** "SSO configuration is
finished and their team completed the core-workflow training session
Tuesday, right on the original timeline."

## 2. Customer sentiment

**What it is.** The customer's actual tone — genuinely positive about how
things are going, or polite-but-frustrated — noting when words and tone
disagree.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a specific tone observation. A
miss looks like sentiment assumed positive with nothing to back it.

**Weak:** "They seem okay with it." **Strong:** "Said things were 'going
fine' but asked twice, with some frustration in tone, why the integration
was taking longer than the original timeline."

## 3. Early engagement signal

**What it is.** Whether the customer is actively participating — asking
questions, testing things themselves — versus passively waiting to be told
what to do next.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a specific example of customer
initiative. A miss looks like engagement level never addressed.

**Weak:** "They're following along." **Strong:** "Their admin had already
tried configuring two integrations before this call and came with specific
questions about a third."

## 4. Milestone / agenda clarity

**What it is.** Whether there's a clear next milestone both sides agree on,
or onboarding is drifting with no defined finish line.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a named milestone and date. A
miss looks like the call ending with no agreed next step.

**Weak:** "We'll keep working through it." **Strong:** "Go-live for the
full team is set for the 15th, contingent only on the SSO rollout finishing
this week."

## 5. Early expansion or add-on signal

**What it is.** Anything that came up — an adjacent use case, another
team's interest — worth flagging for CS later, even if it's not this call's
focus.

**Covered vs. a miss.** Covered looks like a specific signal named, even in
passing. A miss looks like such signals present in the call but not
captured.

**Weak:** "Nothing else came up." **Strong:** "Their admin mentioned in
passing that the support team has been asking about getting access too —
worth flagging for CS, not a focus of this call."

## Reads-well-too check

Sanity-check against an onboarding you know went smoothly. If it still
reads as needs-attention, loosen the read before reporting.
