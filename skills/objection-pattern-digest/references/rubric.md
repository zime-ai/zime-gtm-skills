# Objection-Pattern-Digest rules

Reads many calls at once. It counts only what the calls show. It needs at least three calls in the input. Within those calls, a theme is a pattern when it appears in at least two of them. It separates a real objection from a question or a minor complaint, and it does not guess a cause.

Three rules apply to every section.

- **Cite or leave it out.** Every claim traces to a quote, a timestamp, or a column and cell. A claim with no cited source does not ship.
- **The buyer or customer must say it.** A claim that only the rep makes does not count unless the other side accepted it. Silence is not a yes.
- **Unknown stays unknown.** When the input does not say it, write "not stated". Do not fill the gap with a guess or with outside knowledge.

## Scope

State how many calls were read and how many contained at least one objection. State any call that was cut off.

## Patterns

Group objections into themes, such as price, past failure, integration, or timing. List a theme as a pattern only when it appears in at least two calls. For each theme give the call count and one quote from each call.

## Resolved or not

For each pattern, state in how many calls the buyer accepted an answer on the call. A buyer who accepts says so. A rep claim that the answer worked does not count.

## What worked

For each pattern, name what the rep did in the calls where the buyer accepted the answer. Quote the rep. Write "no call resolved it" when none did.

## One-call objections

List objections that appear in one call only, with a quote. Say that they may not repeat.

## What does not count

- A question about a feature, written as an objection.
- A minor complaint that did not block or delay the deal.
- A rep claim that an objection was resolved, with no acceptance from the buyer.
- A theme with one call, written as a pattern.
- A cause for an objection that no call states.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize.

- A set of calls with no repeated objection must produce a short digest that says so. Do not group unrelated objections to make a pattern.
- A thin input must not get a padded output. When the input supports two items, write two. Do not invent a third to reach a count.

## Sources

The structure follows common objection-analysis practice. The rules come from reviews of recorded sales calls.
