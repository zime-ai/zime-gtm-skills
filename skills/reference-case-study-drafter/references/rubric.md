# Reference-Case-Study-Drafter rules

Writes the draft from one customer call. The customer must say it. A rep claim that the customer did not accept does not enter the draft. The draft is not ready to publish: the customer must approve it first.

Three rules apply to every section.

- **Cite or leave it out.** Every claim traces to a quote, a timestamp, or a column and cell. A claim with no cited source does not ship.
- **The buyer or customer must say it.** A claim that only the rep makes does not count unless the other side accepted it. Silence is not a yes.
- **Unknown stays unknown.** When the input does not say it, write "not stated". Do not fill the gap with a guess or with outside knowledge.

## Customer's own words

List the customer's lines about the problem, what they changed, and the result, as exact quotes. Use only the customer's lines.

## Draft

Write a draft of 150 words at most. Tag each sentence with the quote that supports it. Do not write a sentence that no customer quote supports.

## Figures that need approval

List every number in the draft with the quote it came from. A figure that only the rep stated, or that the customer called rough, is marked as rough or left out.

## What is missing

List what a case study usually needs and the call lacks, such as a baseline, a result, a time frame, or a quote about why they chose the vendor. Write "not stated" for each.

## Permission

State what the customer said about naming the company or using the story. Write "not stated" if the call is silent. State that the customer must approve the final text.

## What does not count

- A result that only the rep stated.
- A figure the customer called rough, written as exact.
- A benefit that the customer said it cannot support.
- The customer's name used when the customer asked not to be named yet.
- A sentence in the draft with no supporting quote.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize.

- A call where the customer gave a baseline, a result, a time frame, and permission must produce a full draft with few gaps. Do not invent a gap.
- A thin input must not get a padded output. When the input supports two items, write two. Do not invent a third to reach a count.

## Sources

The draft follows common customer-story practice. The rules come from reviews of recorded customer calls.
