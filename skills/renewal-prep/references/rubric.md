# Renewal-Prep rules

Writes the prep from recent customer calls. It counts only what the customer said, treats silence on risk as unknown and not as safe, and separates new scope from more use of what the customer already pays for. It uses nothing from outside the calls.

Three rules apply to every section.

- **Cite or leave it out.** Every claim traces to a quote, a timestamp, or a column and cell. A claim with no cited source does not ship.
- **The buyer or customer must say it.** A claim that only the rep makes does not count unless the other side accepted it. Silence is not a yes.
- **Unknown stays unknown.** When the input does not say it, write "not stated". Do not fill the gap with a guess or with outside knowledge.

## Value, in the customer's words

State the outcome that the customer named, with the figure and the quote. When the customer gave none, write "not stated". The CSM's own claim of value does not count.

## Who uses it

State who uses the product and who does not, with counts when the customer gave them. Note any drop in use that the customer mentioned.

## Risks to the renewal

List risks that the customer's words support: a stated intent to stop or switch, a chronic issue, a budget review, or a champion who left. Cite each. Silence on risk is written as "not asked", and it is not reassurance.

## Open issues and owners

List each open issue with its owner and its date. Include an issue that the customer raised before and never saw closed. An issue with no owner is written as "no owner".

## Expansion signals

List only new scope that the customer wants, such as a new team or product, where money or a date was mentioned. More use inside the current plan is not expansion.

## What to ask and when

State the questions to ask, such as who decides the renewal, the date, and what the new contact thinks. Mark any ask as the CSM's proposal.

## What does not count

- A tone that the writer reads as happy, with no words cited.
- A value claim that only the CSM makes.
- More use inside the current plan, written as expansion.
- A specialist tool that the customer tried for a narrow job, written as a churn risk, unless the customer ties it to the reason they bought.
- A renewal date that nobody on the call stated.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize.

- An account with a named result, steady use, no open issue, and a named decider must produce a short, positive prep. Do not add risks that the calls do not support.
- A thin input must not get a padded output. When the input supports two items, write two. Do not invent a third to reach a count.

## Sources

The structure follows common renewal-planning practice. The rules come from reviews of recorded customer calls.
