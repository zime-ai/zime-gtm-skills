# Note format — the output contract

A prep note has exactly four parts, in this order. Nothing above the hook,
nothing after the footer.

1. **Hook** (3-second read)
2. **🎯 Win condition** (one sentence)
3. **Your 3 moves** (10-second read)
4. **Watch out for** (below the fold — this is the OSS stand-in for the
   AMA deep-link the hosted product uses; here it's just printed)

No em-dashes anywhere in the note. Use exact names — people, companies,
products — never "the prospect" or "the stakeholder" once a name is known.

## 1. Hook

One of three states, chosen by what the evidence actually shows — never
invent a risk to make the hook punchier.

- **Risk.** A live objection or blocker exists in the transcript(s). Bold
  title naming it crisply, then up to 2 short lines: the specific objection
  and the consequence if it's not addressed today. Cite it:
  `("we can't justify that price to procurement," Mar 12 call)`.
  Example title: "Risk: Priya can't defend the price internally."
- **No risk.** No live objection found, momentum looks fine. One line saying
  so, plus the one thing to confirm today to keep it that way. No manufactured
  alarm just to fill the slot.
- **No history yet.** No transcript was available for this account/deal.
  Say so plainly — "No call history yet for this account" — don't guess a
  first-call story if the meeting title suggests an ongoing relationship
  (recurring, sync, check-in, weekly, biweekly).

## 2. Win condition

`🎯 **Win condition:** ` (label, colon, one space) then one sentence, 25 words
max. What does winning *this call* mean, not the deal. Tied to a named person
and a concrete result.

Example: `🎯 **Win condition:** Priya leaves with a two-line pricing summary
she can forward to procurement without a follow-up call.`

## 3. Your 3 moves

Exactly 3, numbered with literal, increasing numerals `1.` `2.` `3.` — write
them out; don't rely on markdown auto-increment, and never repeat `1.`. No
bullets, the number is the marker.

Each move: **bold lead** (the move itself, a few words) + optional one-clause
detail. Hard cap 20 words total for lead + detail. The move must be a
commitment with a checkable result, not a vague suggestion ("build rapport"
is not a move; "get a named integration owner and a date" is).

Moves come from, in priority order:
1. A live objection from the hook, if resolving it is what moves this deal/
   account forward.
2. The exit criteria of the account's current stage (see `stage-criteria.md`
   or the user's own config) — these double as what gets scored after the
   call, so the moves and the scorecard are the same list.
3. Decision-process and timeline gaps visible in the transcript(s) (who signs,
   what's the next date).

No links, no "how top reps do this" — that's the hosted product's AMA
deep-link; there's no AMA here to open.

Example:
```
1. **Lock the integration owner.** Get one named owner for the Dynamics sync.
2. **Set a date for the pilot review.** Turn "next few weeks" into a calendar hold.
3. **Confirm who signs.** Name the budget approver before the deal stalls on it.
```

## 4. Watch out for

Up to 3 stage-typical traps or risks worth flagging, each with a citation if
it's a claim about something said, or none if it's general stage guidance.
Cap at 3 short lines — this is a below-the-fold scan, not a report.

If a competitor was named and isn't already the hook, name it here with the
one thing that beats them on this account.

## Internal / personal calls — no note

If every attendee shares the user's own company domain (or the meeting is
plainly personal — 1:1, no external party), do not generate parts 1–4.
Output exactly: `No prep note. This looks like an internal call.` and stop.
