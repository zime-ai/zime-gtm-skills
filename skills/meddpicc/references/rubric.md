# MEDDPICC rubric

Eight letters — MEDDICC plus Paper process. A call earns "Not applicable"
rather than "Missed" when the letter genuinely wasn't reachable at this
point in the deal — that distinction matters more here than in a
single-stage rubric, because MEDDPICC is meant to be read cumulatively
across a deal's whole call history, not perfectly in any one call.

## Metrics

**What it is.** Whether the prospect attached a number to the value of
solving this — cost saved, time saved, revenue protected or unlocked — in
their own words or something close to it, tied to a business consequence
if the problem stays unsolved.

**Covered looks like:** a specific figure or range volunteered unprompted,
or returned to more than once, tied to a real cost, risk, or missed
outcome. **Partial looks like:** a number is mentioned but stays vague
("this would help a lot") or is only produced when the rep asks directly.
**Missed looks like:** no financial or operational consequence is ever
named, or the prospect can't connect the problem to any measurable cost.

"This would help a lot" is Partial; "we lose about $30k a quarter chasing
this manually" is Covered.

## Economic buyer

**What it is.** Whether the person who can actually approve spend is
identified — ideally on the call or referenced by name and title with a
described path to them — not just "I'll need to check with someone."

**Covered looks like:** the economic buyer is named, their approval
threshold or authority is described, and there's a plan to get them
directly engaged. **Partial looks like:** the buyer is named but hasn't
been on a call or engaged directly, and their alignment to the deal is
relayed secondhand rather than confirmed. **Missed looks like:** no one on
the call can say who approves spend, or the only answer is "someone above
me" with no name attached.

"My VP owns this budget" without naming them is Partial; naming the VP,
their approval threshold, and confirming they're bought into the pain
(even without being on the call yet) is Covered.

## Decision criteria

**What it is.** What the prospect's organization actually evaluates on —
technical fit, price, vendor stability, integration effort — and which of
those are must-haves versus nice-to-haves.

**Covered looks like:** specific evaluation criteria are named, with some
sense of which matter most and why. **Partial looks like:** a general
preference is stated ("we care about ease of use") without ranking or
detail on what "ease of use" would need to look like. **Missed looks
like:** the call never surfaces what the prospect is actually evaluating
against, leaving the rep guessing at what to emphasize later.

"We want something reliable" is Partial; "integration effort matters more
to us than price, because a past vendor's rollout ran four months over
what they quoted" is Covered.

## Decision process

**What it is.** The steps, order, and rough timeline from "interested" to
"signed" — who recommends, who approves, roughly when. Distinct from
Decision criteria (the *what* is being evaluated) — this is the *how* and
*when* the deal moves internally. Distinct from Paper process below — this
is the internal path to a yes; Paper process is what happens once that yes
exists.

**Covered looks like:** the approval sequence is named end to end (who
recommends, who approves, any remaining formality) with a rough sense of
timing. **Partial looks like:** one or two steps are named but the
sequence or timing stays vague. **Missed looks like:** no one can describe
how a decision like this actually gets made inside the account.

"I recommend, my boss approves the budget, and then it goes to our ops
committee — mostly a formality" is Covered; "it'll go through a few people"
is Partial.

## Paper process

**What it is.** How concretely the deal is progressing through the formal
purchasing steps between a verbal yes and a signed contract — legal
review, procurement, security review, PO issuance — and who owns each one.
This is what MEDDPICC adds over MEDDICC, and it earns its place as a
separate letter from Decision process: a deal can have a clean, well-mapped
internal decision process and still stall for weeks once it hits legal
redlines, a procurement cycle, or a security questionnaire nobody scoped
for.

**Covered looks like:** procurement activities (legal review, contract
review, security review, vendor onboarding) are already underway, with
owners and a target date for the next milestone. **Partial looks like:**
the path is understood in general terms (there's a legal team, there's
probably a security review) but nothing has formally started and no owner
or date is attached. **Missed looks like:** the call never raises any of
this on a deal that's late-stage enough that paperwork should already be
on the table — not Not applicable, unless the call genuinely predates any
close discussion. Look for signal across contract path (who reviews first,
redline history with this vendor type), procurement involvement (a formal
function in the loop, PO thresholds that trigger extra sign-off), security
or compliance review (a questionnaire, a SOC 2/ISO request, typical
turnaround), signature authority (who actually signs, often not the
economic buyer), and timeline realism (has anyone put a date on how long
their own process typically takes).

"I haven't thought that far ahead — I couldn't tell you who or how long
that usually takes" is Missed on a deal three weeks past verbal approval;
"legal is already reviewing the MSA, security wants a SOC 2 report by next
Friday, and our VP of Ops signs anything over $75k" is Covered.

## Identify pain

**What it is.** The specific, quantifiable business pain driving this
evaluation — the story behind the Metrics number, not a generic
frustration, acknowledged as real and relevant in the prospect's own words.

**Covered looks like:** the pain is urgent, tied to a clear business
impact, and something the prospect returns to unprompted. **Partial looks
like:** a real problem is named once without detail on cause or ongoing
impact. **Missed looks like:** only category-level interest surfaces, with
no specific problem the prospect owns.

"We're always looking to improve" is Missed; "our dispatch board and
carrier portal don't talk to each other, so freight gets double-booked" is
Covered.

## Champion

**What it is.** Someone inside the account who will sell on the rep's
behalf when the rep isn't in the room — actively, not just someone who
likes the product. At a late stage, a strong champion should also be doing
something to move Paper process along, not only advocating on product fit.

**Covered looks like:** the champion is shown taking a concrete action —
looping in a stakeholder, relaying a number to leadership, flagging a
legal or procurement contact. **Partial looks like:** someone speaks
positively about the deal but there's no evidence they've done anything to
move it internally. **Missed looks like:** no one on the call is acting as
an internal advocate at all.

Someone saying "I really like where this is going" with no follow-through
is Partial; someone who took the cost number to their own leadership and
got buy-in is Covered.

## Competition

**What it is.** What else the prospect is evaluating, formally or
informally, and how the rep's solution is being framed against it —
including "doing nothing" or staying with the status quo, which is often
the real competitor in early- and late-stage deals alike.

**Covered looks like:** a specific alternative is named, with some sense
of how seriously it's being compared or why it's been ruled out.
**Partial looks like:** the prospect mentions having looked at options
without naming any or describing how the comparison went. **Missed looks
like:** competition never comes up despite the deal plausibly being
contestable.

"We looked at one competitor early on but didn't get far enough to compare
seriously" is Partial; naming that competitor and what tipped the
evaluation away from them is Covered.

## Reads-well-too check

A call that is genuinely strong on the letters it *could* address at this
stage should not come back mostly Missed just because letters unreachable
at this point (e.g. Paper process before a deal has reached late-stage
discussion) get scored as gaps rather than Not applicable. If that happens,
re-score using Not applicable for anything the call's stage couldn't
reasonably have surfaced.
