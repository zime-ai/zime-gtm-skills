# MEDDPICC rubric

Eight letters — MEDDICC plus Paper process. A call earns "Not applicable"
rather than "Missed" when the letter genuinely wasn't reachable at this
point in the deal — that distinction matters more here than in a
single-stage rubric, because MEDDPICC is meant to be read cumulatively
across a deal's whole call history, not perfectly in any one call.

## Metrics

Did the prospect attach a number to the value of solving this — cost saved,
time saved, revenue protected or unlocked — in their own words or something
close to it? "This would help a lot" is Partial; "we lose about $30k a
quarter chasing this manually" is Covered.

## Economic buyer

Is the person who can actually approve spend identified, and — ideally — on
the call or referenced by name and title, not just "I'll need to check with
someone"? A champion saying "my VP owns this budget" without naming them is
Partial.

## Decision criteria

What does the prospect's organization actually evaluate on — technical
fit, price, vendor stability, integration effort? A call that never surfaces
this leaves the rep guessing at what to emphasize later.

## Decision process

The steps, order, and rough timeline from "interested" to "signed" —
who recommends, who approves, roughly when. Distinct from Decision criteria
(the *what*) — this is the *how* and *when* the deal moves internally.
Distinct from Paper process below — this is the internal path to a yes;
Paper process is what happens once that yes exists.

## Paper process

The legal, procurement, and security steps between a verbal yes and a
signed contract, and who owns each one. This is what MEDDPICC adds over
MEDDICC, and it earns its place as a separate letter from Decision process:
a deal can have a clean, well-mapped internal decision process and still
stall for weeks once it hits legal redlines, a procurement RFP cycle, or a
security questionnaire nobody scoped for. Look for:

- **Contract path** — MSA/DPA needed, redline history with this vendor or
  vendor type, whose legal team reviews first (buyer's or seller's).
- **Procurement involvement** — a formal procurement function in the loop,
  competitive-bid requirements, PO thresholds that trigger extra sign-off.
- **Security/compliance review** — a security questionnaire, SOC 2/ISO
  request, a formal vendor-risk assessment, and its typical turnaround.
- **Signature authority** — who actually signs (often not the economic
  buyer), and whether that person has been named, not just assumed.
- **Timeline realism** — has anyone put a date on how long their own legal
  and procurement process typically takes for a vendor this size? A rep who
  has this number can forecast; a rep who doesn't is guessing.

A call that never raises any of this is Missed once the deal is late-stage
enough that paperwork should already be on the table — not Not applicable,
unless the call genuinely predates any close discussion.

## Identify pain

The specific, quantifiable business pain driving this evaluation, not a
generic frustration. Overlaps with Metrics but is broader — Metrics is the
number, this is the story behind the number.

## Champion

Someone inside the account who will sell on the rep's behalf when the rep
isn't in the room — actively, not just someone who likes the product. Look
for evidence of the champion doing something (looping in a stakeholder,
pushing internally), not just saying positive things to the rep. At this
stage, a strong champion should also be doing something to move Paper
process — flagging the right legal or procurement contact, not just
advocating on product fit.

## Competition

What else the prospect is evaluating, formally or informally, and how the
rep's solution is being framed against it — including "do nothing" as a
competitor, which is often the real one in early-stage deals.

## Reads-well-too check

A call that is genuinely strong on the letters it *could* address at this
stage should not come back mostly Missed just because letters unreachable at
this point (e.g. Paper process before a deal has reached late-stage
discussion) get scored as gaps rather than Not applicable. If that happens,
re-score using Not applicable for anything the call's stage couldn't
reasonably have surfaced.
