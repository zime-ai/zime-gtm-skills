# Pipeline-Review rules

Reads one export, or two exports to show change. Every number is computed from the rows and shown with the rows it came from. The brief does not explain why a number moved, because an export cannot say why.

Three rules apply to every section.

- **Cite or leave it out.** Every claim traces to a quote, a timestamp, or a column and cell. A claim with no cited source does not ship.
- **The buyer or customer must say it.** A claim that only the rep makes does not count unless the other side accepted it. Silence is not a yes.
- **Unknown stays unknown.** When the input does not say it, write "not stated". Do not fill the gap with a guess or with outside knowledge.

## Snapshot by stage

Show the count and total amount for each stage, and the total. Compute from the rows. State the number of rows that have no amount.

## What changed

When a previous export is given, list deals that are new, moved stage, or left the export, matched by deal name. Without one, write "no previous export". Never infer movement from days in stage.

## Deals that need attention

List at most five deals. Choose by a cited reason: a past close date with probability under 50, a blank or vague next step, a single contact, or no activity for a long time against the export's median. Cite the cells. Rank by amount.

## Data gaps

List columns that are missing and counts of blank cells that block a review, such as a blank amount. Name the deals for any blank amount.

## Questions for the review

Write up to four questions for the meeting. Tie each to a deal or a gap above.

## What does not count

- A reason for a change that the export does not state.
- A trend from one export.
- More than five deals in the attention list.
- A deal marked at risk by feel, with no cited cell.
- A stage total that does not add up from the rows.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize.

- A clean export with no blank cells and no flags must produce a snapshot and a short note that nothing needs attention. Do not pad the attention list.
- A thin input must not get a padded output. When the input supports two items, write two. Do not invent a third to reach a count.

## Sources

The structure follows common weekly pipeline-review practice. The rules come from reviews of exports.
