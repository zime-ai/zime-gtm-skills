# Behavior checklists

Five fixed behaviors this skill scores every call against. Each has one or
more checklist items (`CHx.y`); Step 4 of `SKILL.md` scores each item 1 or 0
per call, with a quote as evidence for every 1.

## BH1: Rapport

Rapport measures the strength of trust, openness, and relationship with the
prospect — it's the foundation of trust in early sales interactions,
reflecting the rep's ability to establish comfort, credibility, and genuine
human connection before diving into transactional discussions. Strong
rapport increases openness, reduces buyer resistance, and improves
information flow, setting the tone for a collaborative rather than
evaluative conversation.

Look for whether the rep moved the conversation beyond formal introductions
and transactional details, and whether the prospect reciprocated with
openness or engagement — personal, contextual, or relational moments (small
talk, shared experiences, customized references, acknowledgment of prior
research) that signal psychological comfort. Evidence of rapport is not just
friendly dialogue, but connection indicators: laughter, relaxed tone,
mirroring of language, or the prospect offering more information than asked
for.

- **CH1.1** — Did the prospect talk about anything beyond transactional
  things in the first 2–5 minutes, opening up to the rep or referencing
  specific research or social proof relevant to this prospect?

## BH2: Upsell opportunities

Identifies potential areas where the prospect or customer may benefit from
additional products or services: challenges unrelated to their current
purchase that other products could address, interest in additional
features, feature requests, competitor product interest, or complementary
tools that could enhance their experience. Interest in additional services
reveals expansion opportunities aligned with the prospect's future needs.

- **CH2.1** — What challenges or issues is the prospect or customer facing
  that are unrelated to what they've already purchased, and that another
  product could address?
- **CH2.2** — What additional features or services has the prospect or
  customer shown enthusiasm for or expressed interest in?
- **CH2.3** — What additional feature requests has the customer raised?
- **CH2.4** — What specific competitor product features or tools has the
  prospect or customer mentioned, indicating potential usage or interest?
- **CH2.5** — What benefits could the prospect's business gain by expanding
  their usage of the product?

## BH3: Renewal challenges

Issues, obstacles, or dissatisfaction points that may impact the prospect's
likelihood to renew — operational, product-related, budgetary, or
organizational challenges that could hinder continuation.

- **CH3.1** — What barriers or challenges does the prospect face in using
  the solution effectively?
- **CH3.2** — What factors or changes will affect the renewal decision?
- **CH3.3** — What changes, upgrades, or resources are needed for renewal?
- **CH3.4** — What feedback does the prospect have to improve their
  experience?

## BH4: Customer experience

The insights, opinions, and experiences the client shares during the call
about their use, satisfaction, challenges, and overall perception of the
product and related services — what's working, what's not, perceived value,
gaps, concerns, and reactions to recommendations or upgrades discussed.

- **CH4.1** — What was the customer's overall emotional tone by the end of
  the call: satisfaction/confidence, neutral or cooperative but uncertain,
  or frustration/confusion/dissatisfaction?

## BH5: Value realization

Whether the customer is successfully using the solution and realizing
meaningful business, operational, or technical value from it — evidence
that the customer has moved beyond implementation or evaluation and can
describe outcomes achieved. Strong value realization indicates the solution
is delivering meaningful impact and provides a foundation for continued
adoption, renewal, and future growth.

- **CH5.1** — Did the champion or economic buyer demonstrate on this call
  that they've reached production and can articulate a specific outcome,
  improvement, or business result the product has delivered, in their own
  words?
- **CH5.2** — Did the conversation provide evidence the solution is
  actively adopted and delivering value (production use, regular usage,
  successful rollout, or integration into business workflows)?
