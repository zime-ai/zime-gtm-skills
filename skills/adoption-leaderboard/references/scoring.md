# Scoring discipline

## Selling vs. buying

Decide from the transcript, never by asking. If your internal reps are the
ones being pitched to — asking the outside party about *their* product,
pricing, or roadmap — your team is evaluating a vendor, not selling that
call is not scored. Drop it, pull the next qualifying call to hold coverage,
and note the drop once. Every other qualifying call is scored as a selling
call.

## Per-item scoring: definitive, not subjective

- Every checklist item mark is exactly **1 or 0**, decided by a literal read
  of the transcript against the item's question. Evidence present means 1;
  absent means 0. No middle value, no partial credit at the item level.
- Decide from transcript evidence only. For each 1, hold one verbatim quote
  as proof; for each 0, the absence in the transcript is the proof.
- Apply the same threshold on every call, so the same data always yields the
  same score.
- Never hedge on an individual mark: no "borderline," "arguably,"
  "subjective," "roughly," "seems," or a confidence qualifier on a per-item
  score. State each plainly with a one-line factual reason.
- Genuine coverage limits (a small sample, a rep on only one call, speakers
  not name-resolvable) are real — note each once, briefly and factually, not
  repeated per cell and not as hand-wringing.

## Rolling item marks up to adoption

Keep every per-item 1/0 as the internal working grid — never displayed, no
matter how the output is framed.

- **Per rep, per behavior**: adoption = the mean, across that rep's calls,
  of (items satisfied in that behavior ÷ items in that behavior on that
  call), as a whole number 0–100.
- **Per rep, overall**: adoption = the same mean pooled across every
  behavior's items. This overall number ranks the leaderboard.

## Edge handling

- **No qualifying external calls**, even after widening — say so plainly and
  state what was tried. Widen first, never ask permission to widen.
- **A rep on only one call** — that call's score is their score; mark them
  `(1 call, low confidence)`.
- **Speaker names without emails** — infer internal vs. external from the
  name, the company in the call title, and how they speak, then state the
  legend inferred; don't stop to confirm. The user can correct it after
  seeing the leaderboard.
- **Uploaded recordings with no attendee metadata** (generic labels like
  "Speaker 1") — don't ask the user to name each rep. Infer identity from
  the transcript where possible; where not possible, still score the calls
  and present a team-level adoption view instead of a per-rep leaderboard,
  and say why. Always produce a result.
- **Context limit reached mid-run** — stop fetching new transcripts, compute
  with the calls already scored, and say how many were covered.
