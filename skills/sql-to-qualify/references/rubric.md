# SQL-to-qualify rubric

Five dimensions that separate a real, worth-pursuing opportunity from an SQL
that's just carrying a form-fill label. This is the gate before
`meeting-to-qualify`'s deeper authority/competition/next-step pass — it asks
whether there's anything here worth that deeper pass at all.

## 1. Contact reachability and role relevance

**What it is.** Whether the person on the call has a real stake in the
problem, or a clear path to whoever does — versus the rep talking to
whoever answered the inbound form with no line to anyone who matters.

**Covered looks like:** the person either holds budget/approval authority
themselves, or names a specific person who does and describes a real path
to them. **Partial looks like:** the person lacks authority but points to a
named approver with a plausible route in. **Missed looks like:** the rep
ends the call with no idea who actually controls this decision.

"I don't have budget authority myself, but my director controls the CX
tools budget and she's already asked me twice this week if we've found
anything" is Partial trending Covered — no authority, but a real, active
path to the person who has it. A contact who can't name anyone else at all
is Missed regardless of how the rest of the call went.

## 2. Pain restated in the prospect's own words

**What it is.** Whether the prospect describes the problem in their own
language on the call — not the rep (or this audit) still leaning on the
inbound form's checkbox reason.

**Covered looks like:** a specific incident or situation, described
unprompted, that explains why they reached out. **Partial looks like:** a
category of problem is named without a specific instance. **Missed looks
like:** the prospect can't restate why they filled out the form at all — a
common false-positive where pain was assumed, not confirmed.

"We had two escalations last month where a rep gave a customer wrong
return policy info and it turned into a whole thing on Twitter" is Covered;
"we're just looking into call monitoring options" with nothing more
specific is Partial at best.

## 3. Budget/urgency plausibility

**What it is.** Not a confirmed number or a locked timeline — a rough fit
check on whether this account could plausibly fund a purchase at a
realistic price point, and whether there's any real reason to move now
versus someday.

**Covered looks like:** a comparable prior spend or range, plus a specific
event driving urgency. **Partial looks like:** one of the two surfaces
without the other. **Missed looks like:** neither surfaces — pure silence
on both is a Missed, not a Partial.

"We spent about $40k last year on a different QA tool" alongside "she
wants something in place before our Q4 peak season" is Covered.

## 4. Competitive awareness surfaced

**What it is.** Whether the call surfaced whether the prospect is already
looking at alternatives — competitors, an internal build, doing nothing —
not whether alternatives happen to exist.

**Covered looks like:** the rep asked directly and got a clear answer,
named or not. **Partial looks like:** the topic came up in passing without
a direct question. **Missed looks like:** the rep never asked at all. Zero
competitive context this early isn't automatically bad — it can mean the
prospect isn't shopping seriously yet — but the call should have at least
asked.

## 5. Concrete next meeting booked

**What it is.** Whether the call ended with a specific next meeting, a
date, and the right attendee(s) named — versus a vague "I'll follow up."

**Covered looks like:** a specific date, time, and named attendees who
matter to the decision. **Partial looks like:** a next step is agreed in
principle without a locked date or full attendee list. **Missed looks
like:** no follow-up is secured at all — often the clearest single tell
that this SQL isn't converting to a worked opportunity yet.

## Reads-well-too check

Before finalizing, sanity-check against a call you already know was a
strong, real SQL. If it still comes back mostly Missed, the read is tuned
too strictly — loosen what counts as evidence before reporting.
