# Sandler submarine rubric

Seven compartments, in order. The submarine's rule is sequential sealing —
each compartment is secured before the rep moves to the next, and skipping
one to reach a later one (e.g. pitching before Pain) is itself a finding,
not just a gap in the skipped compartment. A call earns "Not applicable"
rather than "Missed" when a compartment genuinely wasn't reachable yet
(e.g. Fulfillment on a first discovery call).

## Bonding and Rapport

Did the rep build a real working relationship before getting into
business — not small talk for its own sake, but enough trust that the
prospect will answer honest questions later in the call? Look for the rep
matching the prospect's tone and pace rather than rushing past this.

## Up-Front Contract

Did the rep set explicit mutual expectations before pitching anything —
what this call will cover, how long, and what happens at the end
(including permission to say "no" or "not a fit")? "So today let's cover X
and Y, and at the end we'll both know if it's worth a next step" is
Covered. A call that jumps from rapport straight into questions or a pitch
with no stated agenda is Missed.

## Pain

Did the rep get to a specific, business-level pain — with the prospect
naming it in their own words — before proposing any solution? Generic
frustration ("things are slow") is Partial; a rep pitching features before
pain is surfaced is a Missed here even if pain shows up later, since the
sequence was wrong.

## Budget

Is there a real budget discussion — a number, range, or process the
prospect stated — rather than the rep assuming affordability or skipping
the topic because the prospect seemed enthusiastic? "We have not
discussed cost" said honestly by the rep is more Covered in spirit than a
rep who never raises it at all and proceeds as if budget is settled.

## Decision

Decision-process clarity: who else needs to sign off, and what happens
procedurally after this call — not just "sounds good, I'll follow up."
Distinct from Budget: this is about the path and the people, not the
money.

## Fulfillment

Did the rep frame next steps as tentative and conditional on real fit
("if this makes sense for you, here's what a next step could look like")
rather than assuming the sale and pushing a hard close? A rep who declares
next steps as settled without checking the prospect actually agrees is
Missed here even if the prospect goes along with it.

## Post-Sell

A real step to reinforce or reverse the decision after commitment — asking
what could still cause the prospect to back out, or confirming the
decision feels solid — not a throwaway "any other questions?" A call that
ends on logistics alone without touching this is Missed.

## Reads-well-too check

A call genuinely strong on the compartments it *could* reach at this stage
should not read as mostly Missed just because later compartments (Decision,
Fulfillment, Post-Sell) were unreachable on, say, a first call. Re-score
those as Not applicable rather than Missed when the call's stage couldn't
have plausibly reached them.
