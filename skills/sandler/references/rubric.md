# Sandler submarine rubric

Seven compartments in submarine order. The submarine's premise is sequential
sealing — each compartment must be secured before the rep moves to the next.
Skipping a compartment to reach a later one (e.g. pitching before Pain) is
a submarine violation, not just a missed item. A compartment earns "Not
applicable" rather than "Missed" when it genuinely wasn't reachable at this
call's stage (e.g. Fulfillment on a first discovery call that never
approached a close).

Three rules apply to every compartment.

- **The buyer must say it.** Evidence is something the buyer said or agreed to. A claim from the rep does not count. Silence is not a yes.
- **Absence is a result.** A compartment that the call never touched is Missed, or Not applicable under the one case above. Do not credit it because the call felt friendly.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each compartment lists the usual false positives.

## 1. Bonding & Rapport

**What it is.** Whether the rep invested time building genuine working
relationship and trust before diving into business — not small talk for its
own sake, but enough mutual respect and comfort that the prospect will
answer honest questions and share real issues later. This is about the rep
matching the prospect's tone and pace, showing respect for their time and
perspective.

**Covered:** the rep and prospect exchange some personal context, match each
other's pace, and show genuine interest in each other as people, not just
as buyer/seller roles. **Partial:** the rep makes some attempt at personal
connection but keeps it brief and transactional, or the prospect stays
guarded. **Missed:** the rep launches straight into business or the prospect
gives short, reluctant answers throughout; no sense of mutual respect or
real conversation.

"How was your week?" answered with "fine" and no follow-up is Partial at
best; matching the prospect's energy level and building on shared context
("I saw on LinkedIn you just got back from the retail ops conference? How
was that?") is Covered.

**Does not count:** small talk that only the rep makes. A "fine" with no follow-up. Politeness.

**A strong call** builds on something the buyer shares, in the buyer's own pace.

## 2. Up-Front Contract

**What it is.** Whether the rep set explicit, mutual expectations before
pitching anything — what topics this call will cover, how long it will take,
and what happens at the end, including clear permission to say "no" or "not
a fit." This protects both parties and ensures the prospect knows they can
decline without social pressure.

**Covered:** the rep states the agenda, duration, and end-point decision
explicitly, and the prospect confirms they're aligned. **Partial:** the rep
covers some elements (agenda, time) but skips others, or the prospect agrees
without confirmation they understand. **Missed:** the call jumps from rapport
straight into questions or a pitch with no agenda stated at all.

"Let's cover X and Y in the next 25 minutes, and at the end we'll both know
honestly whether it's worth a follow-up — no pressure either way" is Covered;
skipping straight to "tell me about your returns process" is Missed.

**Does not count:** an agenda that the rep reads out and the buyer does not accept. A time limit with no permission to say no.

**A strong call** states the agenda, the time, and that either side can say no at the end, then asks the buyer to agree.

## 3. Pain

**What it is.** Whether the rep surfaced a specific, quantified, business
problem — in the prospect's own words, not assumed or raised by the rep —
and pushed past the first vague answer to real impact or urgency. This is
the foundation of the sales conversation; pitching before pain is surfaced
is a submarine violation even if pain appears later.

**Covered:** the prospect names a concrete problem with a business impact
(cost, time, risk, a number they volunteered), described unprompted or
returned to multiple times. **Partial:** a real problem is named once but
without root cause, impact, or urgency attached. **Missed:** only generic
"we're always looking to improve" interest, with no specific problem the
prospect owns and acknowledges.

"We get a lot of returns" is Partial at best; "that costs us about 10 hours
a week across the team re-entering data" is Covered.

**Does not count:** a problem the rep names and the buyer never raised. A complaint about a vendor the buyer uses today. A problem that vanishes once no vendor exists.

**A strong call** asks for the last real example and what it cost.

## 4. Budget

**What it is.** Whether there was a real, substantive discussion of money —
a number, range, or approval process the prospect stated — rather than the
rep assuming affordability or skipping the topic because the prospect seemed
enthusiastic. This is not asking for a PO, just surfacing the financial
reality and constraints.

**Covered:** a figure, range, or approval status is stated with no hesitation
— "we have roughly $40-60k set aside for this this year." **Partial:** budget
exists but is conditional on further approval or stakeholder alignment, or
the prospect is vague about amount while confirming money is available. **Missed:**
no discussion of cost at all, or the prospect states budget is unavailable,
or the rep never raises the topic and proceeds as if affordability is
settled.

"We'd need to find budget" is Partial; "we have roughly $40-60k set aside"
is Covered; saying nothing at all is Missed.

**Does not count:** worry about price on its own. A question about pricing. The rep's own estimate of the deal size.

**A strong call** asks how the buyer paid for similar tools and who approves the spend.

## 5. Decision

**What it is.** Whether the rep mapped out who else needs to sign off and
what the approval process looks like — names, titles, timeline, how the
person on the call influences the decision. Distinct from Budget (which is
money), this is about the path and the people making the call.

**Covered:** the economic buyer or other approvers are named specifically,
with some sense of the sequence and what they need to see. **Partial:**
another stakeholder is referenced vaguely ("my team would need to weigh in")
or the process is unclear. **Missed:** the call ends without knowing whether
the person on the call can actually say yes, or who needs to approve.

"I can move this forward but my director signs off on anything over $20k,
and she's usually involved in security reviews" is Covered; "I'll just check
with my director" is Partial; never asking is Missed.

**Does not count:** a job title alone. The buyer must say who has the final word. A person who only recommends is not the approver.

**A strong call** asks who else decides and whether that person will join the next call.

## 6. Fulfillment

**What it is.** Whether the rep framed next steps as tentative and
conditional on real mutual fit — "if this makes sense for you, here's what
a next step could look like" — rather than assuming the sale and pushing a
hard close. This is the difference between offering a path forward and
declaring victory prematurely.

**Covered:** the rep presents next steps as conditional ("if you think this
is worth exploring"), and the prospect affirms genuine interest, not just
polite agreement. **Partial:** the rep frames next steps conditionally but
the prospect's agreement is hesitant or unclear, or the rep declares next
steps as settled but the prospect goes along without clear enthusiasm.
**Missed:** the rep assumes the sale and declares next steps as final without
checking the prospect actually agrees; prospect's hesitant agreement ("I
guess") is evidence the close was assumed, not mutual.

"If this makes sense for you, we could do a follow-up to walk through
implementation" is Covered; declaring "let's get this moving — I'll send
over a contract this week" without checking prospect readiness is Missed.

**Does not count:** "let us get this moving" with no word from the buyer. A buyer who goes along with "I guess".

**A strong call** offers the next step as conditional and waits for the buyer to say they want it.

## 7. Post-Sell

**What it is.** Whether the rep took a real step after apparent agreement to
reinforce the decision or uncover any last hesitation — asking what could
still cause the prospect to back out, confirming the decision feels solid, or
checking for remaining concerns. This is not logistics confirmation ("we'll
send the contract Friday"), but emotional/strategic closure.

**Covered:** the rep asks a genuine reinforcement or reversal question —
"what would need to happen for this to feel like the right decision?" or
"anything that could still cause you to back out?" — and the prospect
responds authentically. **Partial:** the rep makes some attempt to check
commitment but it's brief or perfunctory. **Missed:** the call ends on
logistics alone ("I'll get that to you today") or a throwaway "any other
questions?" with no reinforcing or reversing step.

"Anything that could still cause the prospect to back out?" is Covered;
"any other questions?" is Missed.

**Does not count:** "any other questions?". Logistics alone, such as "I will send the contract today".

**A strong call** asks what could still make the buyer back out and listens to the answer.

## Reads-well-too test

A call genuinely strong on the compartments it could reach at this stage
(e.g. a first call that bonds well, sets an up-front contract, and surfaces
real pain) should not read as mostly Missed just because later compartments
(Decision, Fulfillment, Post-Sell) were unreachable. Re-score those as Not
applicable rather than Missed when the call's stage couldn't have plausibly
reached them. If a genuinely good call still returns several Missed scores,
the rubric is biased toward finding fault — loosen what counts as evidence
before finalizing.
