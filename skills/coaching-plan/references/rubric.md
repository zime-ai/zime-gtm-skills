# Coaching-Plan rules

Writes the plan from the rep's own calls. One call shows an observation. A pattern needs the same gap in at least two calls. The plan names a pattern only when the evidence supports it, and it keeps the list short.

Three rules apply to every section.

- **Cite or leave it out.** Every claim traces to a quote, a timestamp, or a column and cell. A claim with no cited source does not ship.
- **The buyer or customer must say it.** A claim that only the rep makes does not count unless the other side accepted it. Silence is not a yes.
- **Unknown stays unknown.** When the input does not say it, write "not stated". Do not fill the gap with a guess or with outside knowledge.

## Strengths to keep

Name one or two things the rep did well, with a quote from the rep and the buyer's reply that shows it worked. Skip this section when no reply shows it worked.

## The pattern

Name the one or two gaps that appear in at least two calls. Quote each call. When a gap appears in one call only, list it under "One-call observations" and do not call it a pattern.

## One-call observations

List gaps that appear once, with a quote. Say plainly that they may not repeat.

## The question to practice

Write one question for each pattern that the rep can ask next time. Make it a question, not advice.

## A drill

Write one short drill for the pattern, such as replaying a moment and rewording it aloud. Keep it to a few minutes.

## How to check progress

State what the manager looks for in the next call, as a line that can be quoted. Name the call.

## What does not count

- A gap seen in one call, written as a pattern.
- A trait of the rep, such as confidence or energy, with no quote.
- More than two patterns.
- A strength that the buyer's reply does not support.
- Advice that is not tied to a quote from the calls.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize.

- A rep with no repeated gap must get a short plan that says so. Do not invent a pattern to give the manager something to say.
- A thin input must not get a padded output. When the input supports two items, write two. Do not invent a third to reach a count.

## Sources

The structure follows common call-coaching practice. The rules come from reviews of recorded sales calls.
