# Next-step-commitment rubric

This rubric has three elements. All three must be present for a Real commitment verdict. When any one is missing, the verdict drops to Weak. When all three are missing, or the call ends with nothing like a next step, the verdict is No commitment.

Three rules apply to every element.

- **Quote the end of the call.** Read the last few minutes first. A next step that appears only in the middle of the call, and gets dropped at the close, does not count.
- **The buyer must accept it.** A step that only the rep states does not count until the buyer agrees. Silence is not a yes.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each element lists the usual false positives. Read that line before you mark it present.

## 1. Specific action

**What it is.** A named thing that will happen next. A vague promise to stay in touch is not one. The sales-call equivalent is an assigned task that someone owns.

**Covered looks like:** a demo scheduled, a proposal sent for review, an intro to a named stakeholder, or a signed document reviewed by legal. **Missed looks like:** "follow up", "circle back", or "stay in touch", with no named deliverable behind it.

**Does not count:** "I will send a summary and find a time". "Let us reconnect sometime". A promise by the rep to look into a feature or a security answer, with no owner and no due date.

**A strong call** names the deliverable and who attends.

## 2. Date or concrete trigger

**What it is.** An actual calendar date and time. Or an event with an obvious occurrence. Either side can point to it and say "that happened" or "that has not happened yet".

**Covered looks like:** a named date and time, or a trigger such as "once procurement responds to the security questionnaire". **Missed looks like:** "soon", "next quarter", or "early next year" with nothing more specific attached.

**Does not count:** "same time next week" with no agreed day, unless the call already sets a weekly slot. A date on the rep's own task, while the buyer's step has no date.

**A strong call** proposes a specific day and time before it ends, with an agenda.

## 3. Two-sided ownership

**What it is.** The buyer commits to do something too. It can be joining a scheduled call, bringing in a stakeholder, or reviewing a document by a date. The weak case is a next step that only the rep must carry out.

**Covered looks like:** the buyer names a specific thing they will do and by when. **Missed looks like:** the whole next step belongs to the rep. It is a to-do that the rep gave themselves and not a mutual commitment.

**Does not count:** "we will discuss it internally" and "we will come back to you". Both are hopes and not commitments. Being ready is not the same as committing.

**A strong call** asks the buyer to name their own step and the date, then repeats it back.

"I will send you a proposal and you can take a look" is Missed on this element. No ownership goes back to the buyer. "I will send it Thursday. Can you get me a yes or no by early next week so we lock the demo date?" is Covered. The buyer now owns a deliverable with a deadline.

## Verdicts

- **Real commitment:** all three present.
- **Weak commitment:** one or two present. Name exactly which are missing.
- **No commitment:** the call ends without anything like a next step, or with something so vague that none of the three elements is present.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize.

- A call that clearly ended in a scheduled, dated, two-sided next step must not come out Weak because of an overly literal reading. Example: the date was implied by "same time next week" and not by a calendar date. Loosen the reading in that case.
- A call that ended with a hope must not come out Real. When a verdict rests on a line from a "does not count" list, remove that element.

## Sources

The three elements follow common deal-progression practice. The evidence rules and the "strong call" lines come from reviews of recorded sales calls.
