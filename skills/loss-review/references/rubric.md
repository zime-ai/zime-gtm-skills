# Loss-Review rubric

This rubric has seven dimensions. They show whether a debrief with a lost buyer learned the real reason. The goal is learning. It is not a chance to win the deal back.

Three rules apply to every dimension.

- **The buyer must say it.** Evidence is something the buyer said. The rep's theory of why the deal was lost is not evidence. A claim that only the rep makes does not count.
- **Absence is a result.** A dimension that the call never touched is Missed. Do not credit it because the call felt friendly.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each dimension lists the usual false positives.


## 1. Outcome confirmed by the buyer

**What it is.** The buyer states what happened: they chose another vendor, paused, or made no decision.

**Covered:** the buyer states the outcome and names who it went to, or says that nothing was chosen. **Partial:** the outcome is implied in passing. **Missed:** the rep assumes the outcome and never hears it from the buyer.

**Does not count:** a CRM stage. "I heard you went another way" with no reply from the buyer.

**A strong call** asks "Where did you land?" plainly and waits.

## 2. Real reason in the buyer's words

**What it is.** The buyer names the decisive factor in their own words, and the rep probes past the first answer.

**Covered:** the buyer names the decisive factor and the rep asks a follow-up that tests it. **Partial:** a surface reason such as "price" with no probing. **Missed:** no reason surfaces, or only the rep's guess does.

**Does not count:** price worry that the buyer never tied to the outcome. A rep theory the buyer only nods at. "Timing" with nothing behind it.

**A strong call** asks what tipped it, then asks for the answer when the price is equal.

## 3. Chosen alternative and what tipped it

**What it is.** The buyer names the option they chose and what it did better, with a specific.

**Covered:** the buyer names the alternative and a specific capability or step that made the difference. **Partial:** the buyer names the alternative with no specifics. **Missed:** no alternative is named.

**Does not count:** "other options" with nothing behind it. A rival that only the rep names. A claim from a vendor comparison page that the buyer repeats.

**A strong call** asks which capability or step made the difference.

## 4. Who decided and who influenced

**What it is.** The buyer names who had the final word and how others shaped it.

**Covered:** the buyer names the decider and the role of at least one other person. **Partial:** the buyer refers to "the team" or "leadership" with no names. **Missed:** nobody learns who decided.

**Does not count:** a job title alone. A person who recommends is not the decider.

**A strong call** asks who had the final word and who argued for the vendor.

## 5. What would have changed the outcome

**What it is.** The buyer states a specific change that flips the decision.

**Covered:** the buyer names a specific change and the rep asks what that change was worth. **Partial:** a general wish such as "better pricing". **Missed:** the rep never asks.

**Does not count:** a feature that the rep promises and the buyer never asked for. A hypothetical that only the rep raises.

**A strong call** asks for the flip condition in the buyer's words.

## 6. Door left open honestly

**What it is.** The call ends with a real condition and a date for a later conversation, or an honest close.

**Covered:** the buyer names a trigger or date for reconsidering and agrees to a follow-up. **Partial:** "keep in touch" or a trigger with no date. **Missed:** the call ends closed, or the rep never asks.

**Does not count:** "let us stay in touch". A rep promise to follow up with no time given.

**A strong call** asks for a specific week and permission to call.

## 7. Rep learned and did not defend

**What it is.** The rep listens, asks follow-ups, and does not argue the loss or pitch again.

**Covered:** the rep asks follow-ups, repeats back what was heard, and does not argue. **Partial:** some defending, or one follow-up and then a pitch. **Missed:** the rep argues the loss or pitches again.

**Does not count:** a thank-you alone. A recap of the original pitch.

**A strong call** asks what to do differently and repeats back the answer.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize an audit.

- A debrief that captured the real reason, the chosen option, the decider, and a reopen condition must not come back mostly Missed. When it does, widen what counts as evidence.
- A call that never raised a dimension must not get credit for it. When a status rests on a line from a "does not count" list, remove that status.

## Sources

The dimensions follow common win-loss interview practice. The evidence rules and the strong-call lines come from reviews of recorded sales calls.
