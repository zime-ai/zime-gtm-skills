# MEDDICC rubric

Seven letters. A call earns "Not applicable" rather than "Missed" when the
letter genuinely wasn't reachable at this point in the deal — that
distinction matters more here than in a single-stage rubric, because
MEDDICC is meant to be read cumulatively across a deal's whole call history,
not perfectly in any one call.

## Metrics

Did the prospect attach a number to the value of solving this — cost saved,
time saved, revenue protected or unlocked — in their own words or something
close to it? "This would help a lot" is Partial; "we lose about $30k a
quarter chasing this manually" is Covered.

## Economic buyer

Is the person who can actually approve spend identified, and — ideally — on
the call or referenced by name and title, not just "I'll need to check with
someone"? A champion saying "my VP owns this budget" without naming them is
Partial.

## Decision criteria

What does the prospect's organization actually evaluate on — technical
fit, price, vendor stability, integration effort? A call that never surfaces
this leaves the rep guessing at what to emphasize later.

## Decision process

The steps, order, and rough timeline from "interested" to "signed" —
security review, procurement, legal, a committee vote. Distinct from
Decision criteria (the *what*) — this is the *how* and *when*.

## Identify pain

The specific, quantifiable business pain driving this evaluation, not a
generic frustration. Overlaps with Metrics but is broader — Metrics is the
number, this is the story behind the number.

## Champion

Someone inside the account who will sell on the rep's behalf when the rep
isn't in the room — actively, not just someone who likes the product. Look
for evidence of the champion doing something (looping in a stakeholder,
pushing internally), not just saying positive things to the rep.

## Competition

What else the prospect is evaluating, formally or informally, and how the
rep's solution is being framed against it — including "do nothing" as a
competitor, which is often the real one in early-stage deals.

## Reads-well-too check

A call that is genuinely strong on the letters it *could* address at this
stage should not come back mostly Missed just because letters unreachable at
this point (e.g. Decision process on a first call) get scored as gaps rather
than Not applicable. If that happens, re-score using Not applicable for
anything the call's stage couldn't reasonably have surfaced.
