# MEDDICC rubric

This rubric has seven letters. A call earns "Not applicable" and not "Missed" only in one case. The call is clearly earlier in the deal than the point where the letter can come up. A first discovery call and the Decision process letter are an example. Any other absence is Missed. A MEDDICC audit reads best across a deal's whole call history, and any single call can leave letters open.

Three rules apply to every letter.

- **The buyer must say it.** Evidence is something the buyer said. A claim from the rep does not count. A fact the rep reads out of the CRM does not count either, unless the buyer agrees during the call. A job title is not authority.
- **Absence is a result.** A letter that the call never touched is Missed, or Not applicable under the one case above. Do not credit a letter because the rep seemed prepared. Do not guess from context.
- **Read the "does not count" line.** Each letter lists the usual false positives. Read that line before you write Covered or Partial.

## Metrics

**What it is.** The quantified business impact of solving or not solving the problem. It can be revenue exposure, cost savings, time freed up, or urgency tied to a number that the buyer acknowledges.

**Covered looks like:** a buyer-stated figure tied to the impact of the problem. Example: "we are losing $18k monthly to manual workarounds" or "a 10% revenue miss from this gap". **Partial looks like:** the impact is vague ("this is costing us" with no number). Or the rep calculated the figure and the buyer did not offer it. **Missed looks like:** no amount or measurable consequence surfaced, or only generic interest in the category.

**Does not count:** a number from the rep's own case study. A figure the rep supplies and the buyer only nods at. Price worry, which is a budget signal and not a metric.

**A strong call** asks where the number comes from and who tracks it.

"This would help us streamline" is Partial. "We are spending close to $18,000 a month in overtime on manual exception handling" is Covered.

## Economic buyer

**What it is.** The person who holds final purchase authority or budget approval power. This person is not a champion and not an influencer. This person can say yes or no to the spend. The best evidence names the person, gives the role, and says what that person needs to see before approving.

**Covered looks like:** the economic buyer is named and the buyer describes the approval threshold or process. Example: "Renee signs off on anything over $50k annually". **Partial looks like:** the economic buyer is named but has never joined a call, or the approval power is vague. Example: "I need to check with someone higher up" with no name. **Missed looks like:** no economic buyer is identified, or the rep guessed the budget holder from a title.

**Does not count:** a job title alone. The buyer must say who has the final word. A person whom others wait on for sign-off is the economic buyer, not the champion. A person who only recommends is not the economic buyer.

**A strong call** asks whether the approver will join the next call and what that person needs to see.

"I can move this forward, but my VP signs off on anything over $20k" is Partial. "Our CFO, Renee, is the final approver, and she needs to see the integration timeline before she commits" is Covered.

## Decision criteria

**What it is.** The specific factors that the buyer's organization uses to judge vendors. Examples are technical fit, price against performance, vendor stability, integration effort, and compliance. This is the "what" they judge on, not the "how" they decide.

**Covered looks like:** the buyer names specific factors tied to their situation. Example: "price matters, but the bigger thing is whether it integrates with our WMS without a six-month project". **Partial looks like:** one general preference ("we care about ease of use") with no depth on how the buyer measures it. **Missed looks like:** no criteria surfaced, so the rep has to guess what to stress.

**Does not count:** a reaction to a demo ("that looks good"). A list of features the rep chose to show. Criteria that the rep states and the buyer does not accept.

**A strong call** asks the buyer to rank the criteria and name the must-haves.

"We will look at whatever is most popular" is Partial. "We are evaluating on integration simplicity because our last vendor integration took six months and derailed our go-live" is Covered.

## Decision process

**What it is.** The steps, approvals, and timeline from "interested" to "contract signed". Examples are security review, legal review, a procurement committee, CFO sign-off, and board approval. This is the "how" and "when". It is separate from the criteria.

**Covered looks like:** the buyer describes an ordered sequence with owners and a timeline. Example: "it goes to Marcus and Priya for a recommendation, then to Renee for approval, then to legal for paperwork, about four weeks in total". **Partial looks like:** a rough process ("we have approvals to go through") with no steps and no timing. **Missed looks like:** no process described, or "it is just me deciding" on a deal large enough to need escalation.

**Does not count:** a date for the next meeting. A hypothetical, such as "how long does a typical evaluation take?".

**A strong call** works backward from the buyer's decision date through each step and says whether the date is realistic.

"We will shop it around and see what people think" is Partial. "After your technical demo, it goes to our IT steering committee, then procurement, then Renee, about three weeks for that cycle" is Covered.

## Identify pain

**What it is.** The specific business problem that the buyer faces today and calls real in their own words. The rep did not invent it. It is not general category interest. It includes the root cause, the business impact, and a signal of urgency.

**Covered looks like:** the buyer describes a specific problem tied to business impact without prompting. Example: "we patch workarounds by hand because the tooling cannot handle our exceptions, and it eats about 10% of our operations team's time". **Partial looks like:** a real problem named once with no cause and no impact detail ("we need better tools"). **Missed looks like:** category-level interest only ("we are always looking to improve operations") with no specific problem that the buyer owns.

**Does not count:** a problem that the rep names and the buyer never raised. A complaint about a vendor the buyer uses today, which is an objection. A problem that vanishes once no vendor exists.

**A strong call** asks for the last real example of the problem and then asks who feels it most.

"We would like something faster" is Partial. "Every quarter we lose a manager to manual exception handling for about two weeks because our current system does not handle edge cases" is Covered.

## Champion

**What it is.** A person inside the account who advocates for the deal while the rep is absent. This person does more than like the product. This person pushes it forward or influences other stakeholders.

**Covered looks like:** the buyer or another participant describes the champion doing work. Example: "Priya keeps pushing me on this" or "she already mentioned it to the steering committee". **Partial looks like:** someone is positive about the solution but nobody shows them selling it internally ("Marcus likes the product"). **Missed looks like:** no internal advocate, or only the person on the call with no sign of influence on others.

**Does not count:** friendliness, enthusiasm, or seniority. Action defines a champion. A person whom others wait on for sign-off is the economic buyer, not the champion.

**A strong call** asks what the champion has already done for the deal and what the champion needs from the rep to do more.

"The operations manager thinks this is cool" is Partial. "Priya has pushed for this internally for weeks, and she will talk it up if anyone asks" is Covered.

## Competition

**What it is.** The alternatives that the buyer actively compares or considers. These are other vendors or internal solutions. The letter covers what is under evaluation and how the rep's solution looks against the alternatives. A real "do nothing" option counts as a competitor.

**Covered looks like:** the buyer names a competitor, evaluated it or saw a demo, and describes the tradeoff. Example: "we demoed TechCorp last month, but they felt built for a much bigger company than us". **Partial looks like:** casual mentions ("we know of a few options") with no detail on who or why. **Missed looks like:** no competitor named, or the buyer already chose another vendor and closed the door.

**Does not count:** an evaluation that ended long ago, unless the buyer says what it taught them. "Other options" with nothing behind it is Partial.

**A strong call** asks which option the buyer prefers today and what gap that option has. It then turns the gap into a yes-or-no test.

"We are thinking about a few options" is Partial. "We demoed one other vendor last month, and it felt built for a much bigger company than us, with lots of features we would never use" is Covered.

## Reads-well-too test

Run this test in both directions before you finalize an audit.

- A call that is strong on the letters it can address must not come back mostly Missed. Suppose the audit marks letters as gaps that the call's stage cannot reach. Then score them Not applicable instead.
- A call that never raised a letter must not get credit for it. When a status rests on a line from a "does not count" list, remove that status.

## Sources

The seven letters follow the MEDDICC qualification framework that sales teams widely teach. The evidence rules and the "strong call" lines come from reviews of recorded sales calls.
