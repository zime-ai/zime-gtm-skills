# MEDDICC rubric

Seven letters. A call earns "Not applicable" rather than "Missed" when the
letter genuinely wasn't reachable at this point in the deal — that
distinction matters more here than in a single-stage rubric, because
MEDDICC is meant to be read cumulatively across a deal's whole call history,
not perfectly in any one call.

## Metrics

**What it is.** The quantified business impact or financial consequence of
solving (or not solving) the prospect's problem — revenue exposure, cost
savings, time freed up, or urgency tied to a specific number the prospect
themselves acknowledges.

**Covered looks like:** a prospect-stated figure tied to the problem's impact
— "we're losing $18k monthly to manual workarounds" or "a 10% revenue miss
from this gap." **Partial looks like:** impact acknowledged but vague —
"this is costing us" without a number, or a figure the rep calculated rather
than the prospect offering it. **Missed looks like:** no dollar amount or
measurable consequence surfaced at all, or only generic interest in the
category.

"This would help us streamline" is Partial; "we're spending close to $18,000
a month in overtime on manual exception handling" is Covered.

## Economic buyer

**What it is.** The person who holds final purchase authority or budget
approval power — not a champion, not an influencer, but the one who can
actually say yes or no to spend. Identified by name and role, and ideally
with some signal of what they need to see to approve.

**Covered looks like:** the economic buyer is named and their approval
threshold or process is described — "Renee signs off on anything over $50k
annually." **Partial looks like:** the economic buyer is named but never on
a call, or their approval power is vague — "I'll need to check with someone
higher up" without naming them. **Missed looks like:** no economic buyer
identified at all, or only budget-holder-by-title guessed rather than named.

"I can move this forward but my VP signs off on anything over $20k" is
Partial; "Our CFO, Renee, is the final approver, and she needs to see the
integration timeline before she'll commit" is Covered.

## Decision criteria

**What it is.** The specific factors the prospect's organization will use to
evaluate vendors — technical fit, price-to-performance, vendor stability,
integration effort, compliance requirements. The "what" they evaluate on,
not the "how" they'll do it.

**Covered looks like:** the prospect names specific evaluation factors tied
to their situation — "price matters, but the bigger thing for us is whether
it integrates with our WMS without a six-month project." **Partial looks
like:** a general preference stated once — "we care about ease of use" without
depth on how that'll be measured. **Missed looks like:** no evaluation criteria
surfaced, leaving the rep guessing what to emphasize.

"We'll look at whatever's most popular" is Partial; "We're evaluating on
integration simplicity because our last vendor integration took six months
and derailed our go-live" is Covered.

## Decision process

**What it is.** The sequence of steps, approvals, and timeline the prospect
will follow from "interested" to "contract signed" — security review, legal
review, procurement committee, CFO sign-off, board approval. The *how* and
*when*, distinct from the criteria they evaluate on.

**Covered looks like:** the prospect describes a clear sequence with timeline
— "It goes to Marcus and Priya for a recommendation, then to Renee for
approval, then to legal for paperwork — we're looking at four weeks total."
**Partial looks like:** a rough process mentioned — "we have approvals to go
through" — but without detail on steps or timing. **Missed looks like:** no
process described, or "it's just me deciding" on a deal large enough to need
escalation.

"We'll shop it around and see what people think" is Partial; "After your
technical demo, it goes to our IT steering committee, then procurement,
then Renee — typically three weeks for that cycle" is Covered.

## Identify pain

**What it is.** The specific, concrete business problem the prospect faces
today and acknowledges as real in their own words — not a challenge the rep
invented or assumed, and not generic category interest. Includes root cause,
business impact, and urgency signal.

**Covered looks like:** the prospect unprompted describes a specific problem
tied to business impact — "We're manually patching workarounds on our system
because the existing tooling won't handle our exceptions. It's eating up 10%
of our operations team's time." **Partial looks like:** a real problem named
once without cause or impact detail — "We need better tools." **Missed looks
like:** only category-level interest — "We're always looking to improve
operations" — with no specific problem the prospect owns.

"We'd like something faster" is Partial; "Every quarter we lose a manager to
manual exception handling for about two weeks — our current system just
doesn't handle edge cases" is Covered.

## Champion

**What it is.** A person inside the account who will actively advocate for
the deal internally when the rep isn't in the room — not just someone who
likes the product, but someone showing evidence of pushing it forward or
influencing internal stakeholders.

**Covered looks like:** the prospect or someone else on the call demonstrates
the champion doing work — "Priya's been the one nudging me on this for weeks"
or "She's already mentioned it to the steering committee." **Partial looks
like:** someone who's positive about the solution but no evidence of them
actively selling it internally — "Marcus likes the product." **Missed looks
like:** no identified internal advocate, or only the immediate call
participant with no indication of influence on others.

"The operations manager thinks this is cool" is Partial; "Priya's been the
one pushing for this internally for weeks — she'd probably talk this up if
anyone asked her" is Covered.

## Competition

**What it is.** Alternative vendors or internal solutions the prospect is
actively comparing or considering — their competitive landscape, what's
under evaluation, and how the rep's solution is being framed against the
alternatives. Includes "do nothing" as a competitor when it's a real option.

**Covered looks like:** the prospect names a specific competitor, has evaluated
or demoed them, and describes the tradeoff — "We demoed TechCorp last month,
but they felt built for a much bigger company than us." **Partial looks like:**
competitors mentioned casually — "We're aware of a few options" — without
detail on who or why they matter. **Missed looks like:** no competitors named,
or the prospect has already chosen another vendor and closed the door.

"We're thinking about a few options" is Partial; "We demoed one other vendor
last month — honestly it felt built for a much bigger company than us, lots
of features we'd never use" is Covered.

## Reads-well-too check

A call that is genuinely strong on the letters it *could* address at this
stage should not come back mostly Missed just because letters unreachable at
this point (e.g. Decision process on a first call) get scored as gaps rather
than Not applicable. If that happens, re-score using Not applicable for
anything the call's stage couldn't reasonably have surfaced.
