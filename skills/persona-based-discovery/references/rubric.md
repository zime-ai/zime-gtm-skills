# Persona-adaptation rubric

Five dimensions of whether a discovery call was tailored to the specific
buyer persona(s) in the room, not run as a one-size-fits-all script. Score
each persona present on the call separately where the call had more than
one — a call can nail this for the economic buyer and miss it entirely for
the technical evaluator on the same line.

## 1. Persona correctly identified

Did the rep establish, early in the call, who they were actually talking
to — role, team, and what that role is on the hook for — rather than
assuming it from the meeting title or a LinkedIn glance? "Walk me through
your role and what you're responsible for here" landing a real answer is
Covered. A call that never establishes this and just pitches is Missed,
regardless of how good the pitch was.

## 2. Pain framed in that persona's language

Once the persona is known, did the rep frame the problem in terms that
persona actually cares about, or fall back to generic pitch language? A
technical evaluator hearing "this will reduce your integration overhead and
on-call load" is Covered; the same rep telling an economic buyer the exact
same sentence instead of translating to cost or risk is Missed for that
persona. Reusing one framing verbatim across two different personas on the
same call is itself a signal of a script, not adaptation.

## 3. Persona-appropriate proof point offered

Did the rep back the pitch with the kind of proof that persona would find
credible — technical depth (architecture, API, security posture) for an
evaluator, ROI or business-case numbers for an economic buyer, day-to-day
workflow or time-saved for an end user? Offering an ROI deck to a technical
evaluator who asked how the API handles auth, or vice versa, is a Partial
at best — the proof point exists but was aimed at the wrong audience.

## 4. Persona-specific objections surfaced and addressed

Did the rep draw out and directly address the hesitation that persona
specifically would have — an evaluator's concern about implementation
lift or security review, an economic buyer's concern about payback period
or switching cost, an end user's concern about workflow disruption? A call
where objections never surface may mean there were none, or may mean the
rep didn't probe — mark Unclear rather than assuming either.

## 5. Next step framed for that persona to act on

Is the proposed next step something this specific persona can actually
say yes to and move — a technical trial or sandbox access for an
evaluator, a business-case document or exec review for an economic buyer,
a workflow walkthrough for an end user — rather than one generic "let's
set up a follow-up call" offered to everyone regardless of role?

## Reads-well-too check

Before finalizing an audit, sanity-check it against a call you already know
adapted well to its persona(s). If the rubric still returns several Missed
dimensions on a genuinely well-adapted call, the read is biased toward
finding fault — widen what counts as evidence before reporting. Also
check the reverse: a call that never identifies personas at all but scores
well on dimensions 2-5 anyway is a sign the read is inferring adaptation
that wasn't actually there — dimension 1 gates the rest.
