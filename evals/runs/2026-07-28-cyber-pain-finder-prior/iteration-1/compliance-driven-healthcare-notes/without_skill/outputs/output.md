# Northgate Health Partners — Pain Point Analysis
**Contact:** Priya Anand, VP Compliance & Security
**Source:** Rep's raw call notes (not a clean transcript)

## The one thing to lead with

**Priya is personally on the hook to attest, in writing, to something she doesn't believe is true — and she said so twice, unprompted.**

She signs the board attestation *and* the security questionnaire for her biggest customer (a large hospital network she's a vendor to), both of which effectively claim continuous monitoring is in place. Her own words: *"I don't actually know if we're secure, I know if we're compliant, and I'm told those aren't the same thing anymore"* and *"I need something I can point to in the audit and say yes, this is being watched around the clock, and mean it."*

This isn't an abstract "we should improve our security posture" pain. It's a named individual facing personal/professional exposure for signing an attestation she can't honestly stand behind — to her board and to the customer relationship that likely matters most to the business. Lead with closing that gap for her, specifically framed as something she can point to and defend, not as a generic security upgrade.

## Supporting pain points (in priority order)

1. **No actual 24/7 monitoring capability — and everyone in the chain knows it.**
   Security is one person, Marcus, who also runs the helpdesk and doesn't work nights or weekends. When asked what happens at 2am on a Saturday, her own answer was *"nothing happens until Monday morning"* — said with a laugh that "wasn't really a laugh." This is the concrete, literal gap behind the compliance and insurance language.

2. **A named, dated audit finding with a clock on it.**
   Their outside auditor already flagged "insufficient continuous monitoring" as a finding last cycle. She raised this before the rep even asked about pain — it's clearly top of mind. Renewal is 14 months out, so there's a real deadline to show remediation, not just intent.

3. **Cyber insurance is getting more expensive and asking the question directly.**
   The broker's renewal questionnaire specifically asked about 24/7 monitoring capability, which they can't honestly answer yes to. Rising premiums plus a direct question they're currently fudging is a second, independent forcing function beyond the HIPAA audit.

4. **A near-miss that made the risk concrete, not hypothetical.**
   A nurse's laptop was flagged for a PUP six weeks ago and sat unreviewed for 9 days because no one was watching the console. It turned out benign, but her framing — *"if that had been ransomware we'd be reading about ourselves in Becker's"* — shows she's thinking about this in terms of public breach disclosure and reputational damage in a healthcare-trade-press context, not just audit checkboxes.

5. **Vendor risk to their largest customer relationship.**
   They're a vendor to a large hospital network and she has to complete that customer's security questionnaire too. Continuous monitoring is table stakes for that relationship to continue, which ties this directly to revenue/contract risk, not just internal compliance hygiene.

6. **Decision paralysis, not budget constraint — important for how to sell this.**
   Money is already allocated as part of this year's risk remediation plan, so this is not a price objection. Her anxiety is about picking the wrong solution and having it fail to satisfy the auditor next cycle. That means the sales motion should lead with audit-defensibility, clear mapping to the "insufficient continuous monitoring" finding, and reference-ability with similar healthcare/HIPAA customers — not ROI or discounting.

7. **She's compliance/legal, not security, and knows it.**
   She explicitly separates "secure" from "compliant" and defers to being told they aren't the same thing. She needs a solution (and a partner) that translates technical monitoring into language she can defend to a board and to auditors — she can't be expected to independently judge the technical merits.

## What's notably absent

She did **not** raise alert fatigue, SOC analyst burnout, or tool sprawl — unsurprising, since there's no SOC at all, just one generalist. Don't lead with "reduce noise for your security team" messaging; it won't land and may signal you didn't listen. The pitch should be built around *coverage that doesn't exist yet* and *proof she can show an auditor*, not optimizing an existing security operation.

## Recommended opening angle

Lead with closing the attestation gap: position the offering as the thing that lets Priya sign the board attestation and the hospital network's questionnaire and mean it — true 24/7 coverage that maps directly to the auditor's "insufficient continuous monitoring" finding, with documentation/reporting she can hand to both audiences. Treat the insurance questionnaire and the near-miss incident as secondary proof points that reinforce urgency, and treat budget/price as a non-issue — the real sales work is building her confidence that this specific solution will satisfy the *next* audit cycle.
