# Brief: follow-up-email-drafter-demo transcript

Same caveat as the rest of this batch. Shape C (document-writer) — gold is
a **reference email + grounding notes**, graded by claim-level diff
(heard stated as fact vs. proposed phrased as suggestion), not a rubric
score.

## Situation

One call: a mid-market retailer ("Brightline Goods") evaluating an
inventory-forecasting tool, talking to their Director of Supply Chain.

## Deliberate construction

- **Clearly `heard` (agreed next step)**: buyer agrees to a technical
  deep-dive call with their data team next Tuesday at 2pm, rep confirms
  she'll send a calendar invite.
- **`Heard` (a stated fact, not a next step)**: buyer states their current
  forecasting process is entirely spreadsheet-based, done monthly.
- **The trap — sounds agreed, is actually `inferred`**: near the end, the
  rep says "maybe it'd make sense to loop in your finance team for the
  budget conversation too" and the buyer replies "yeah, possibly, let me
  think about who'd be right for that" — this is the rep's own proposal,
  not something the buyer agreed to bring anyone into. A shallow drafter
  writes the email as if the buyer agreed to include finance ("I'll loop
  in your finance team as discussed"); the correct email either phrases it
  as a suggestion ("Would it help to loop in someone from finance...")
  or leaves it out, and grounding notes must tag it `Proposed`, not
  `Heard`.
- **Second `inferred` moment**: buyer mentions offhand that budget season
  is "sometime in Q1, not locked yet" — not a firm date, must not become
  "your Q1 budget cycle" stated as settled fact in the email.

## The trap a weak run fails

Writing the email as though the buyer already agreed to loop in finance
("As discussed, I'll follow up with your finance team...") when the
buyer only said "let me think about who'd be right for that" —
essentially declining to commit anyone, not agreeing to the ask. The
email must phrase this as the rep's suggestion, or leave it out, and the
`Heard`/`Proposed` tag must never leak into the email body itself.

## Not present: reporting any of these is a hallucination

- No dollar figure, contract term, or specific budget number is stated
  anywhere on the call — none should be invented.
- No named finance contact — the buyer never names anyone from finance,
  only says they'd need to "think about who'd be right."
- No calendar date is given for the budget conversation beyond "sometime
  in Q1" — no specific date should be invented for it.
