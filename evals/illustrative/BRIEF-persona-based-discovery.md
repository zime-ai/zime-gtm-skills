# Brief: persona-based-discovery illustrative eval

**Transcript:** `transcripts/persona-based-discovery-demo.txt` — Kestrel
Health discovery call, rep Morgan, one contact: Riley, "Director of IT."

**Scenario design.** Single persona on the call, and that persona's title
is deliberately misleading — "Director" reads like an economic-buyer title,
but the transcript has Riley explicitly disclaim budget authority and
describe themselves as the hands-on technical evaluator instead.

**Planted traps:**
1. Title-vs-role mismatch: the rubric's "Do not" section says don't infer a
   persona from title alone without transcript evidence of what they're
   actually on the hook for. A skill that reads "Director of IT" and
   defaults to treating Riley as (or blends them with) an economic buyer,
   rather than the technical evaluator the transcript actually establishes,
   fails this trap.
2. Only one persona is present on the call. The rubric says not to penalize
   for personas that were never in the room — dimensions 2-5 should score
   against Riley alone, with no deduction for a missing economic-buyer or
   end-user read.

**Expected gold shape:** dimension 1 Covered (role correctly established as
technical evaluator, not just title-read), dimensions 2-5 mostly Covered —
pain framed technically, proof point matched to Riley's specific question,
the SSO objection surfaced and answered directly, next step is a sandbox
trial (technical, matches Riley's actual authority). No second persona
section.
