Call: Anders Foods <> Vendor — First call, new rep
Participants: Sam (rep, vendor, early in ramp), Jamie (Ops Manager, Anders Foods)

Sam: So, tell me about your company.

Jamie: Sure — we're a regional food distributor, mid-size, mostly grocery
chains as customers.

Sam: Got it. Walk me through what happens today when a shipment's
delayed — where's the time actually going?

Jamie: Honestly it's a scramble. Someone has to manually call the customer,
someone else reroutes the driver, and half the time nobody logs it until
the invoice reconciliation catches the discrepancy days later.

Sam: What's driving that specifically — is it a systems gap or more of a
process thing?

Jamie: Mostly no systems talk to each other. Dispatch doesn't know what
sales promised, sales doesn't know what dispatch is dealing with in
real time.

Sam: That's rough. Why look at this now instead of, say, last year?

Jamie: Our biggest customer just added a penalty clause for late deliveries
starting this quarter. That changed the math on this pretty fast.

Sam: That's a real forcing function. So, let me walk you through what we
do — we've got reporting, dispatch integrations, an API for custom
workflows, and a mobile app for drivers.

Jamie: Okay.

Sam: And you mentioned the penalty clause — our exception-alert view is
actually built exactly for catching a delay before it triggers something
like that, flags it the moment a route falls behind schedule.

Jamie: That part's actually relevant, yeah. Actually, that reminds me — we
had a similar contract dispute with a different carrier last year, cost us
a chunk of the account before we caught it.

Sam: That's exactly the kind of thing this is meant to prevent. I'll follow
up with more detail on the exception-alert piece specifically.

Jamie: Sounds good.
