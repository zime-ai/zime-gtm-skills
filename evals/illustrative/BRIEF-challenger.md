## Situation

Mid-market insurance brokerage ("Corvid Underwriting," fictional), ~150
employees. First call between an AE (selling a claims-automation platform)
and the VP of Claims Operations, inbound after downloading a benchmark
report.

## Deliberate construction (by rubric dimension, decided before writing)

- **Teach**: the rep should genuinely reframe the buyer's problem, not just
  restate it — teach that claims cycle-time, not headcount, is the actual
  lever on customer retention (backed by a specific stat), something the
  VP has not connected before.
- **Tailor**: the rep should connect the teaching to something specific
  about this buyer's economics (their book of business skews toward a
  claim type where cycle-time hits retention hardest) — not a generic
  pitch delivered identically to any insurer.
- **Take control**: deliberately weak. The rep should ask a soft,
  permission-seeking question near the end ("would it maybe make sense to
  loop in your team at some point?") rather than proposing a specific next
  step with urgency — a real take-control gap for the skill to catch, not
  papered over.
- **Constructive tension**: the rep should push back once on an assumption
  the VP states as settled (that adding headcount is the only lever),
  directly, not just agree and pivot.
- **Legitimately N/A**: no explicit pricing/negotiation resistance occurs
  on this call — it's a first call, nothing to code as "handled
  resistance" one way or the other. Correct read is Not applicable /
  Not yet reached, not Missed.
- **Downplayed secondary signal**: the VP mentions, almost as an aside,
  that a board member has been asking pointed questions about claims
  automation lately — a real urgency/political signal the VP treats as
  minor color, not the headline.
